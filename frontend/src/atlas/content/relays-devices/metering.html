☰The rules, stated plainly

Revenue metering

  • A revenue meter is a certified accuracy class (SEL-735: ANSI C12.20-2015 Class 0.1; SEL-734: Class 0.2). Relay metering is a diagnostic — never bill from it.
  • Everything lands in one of four P/Q quadrants — delivered/received × leading/lagging — and each quadrant has its own energy registers.
  • Energy accumulates forever, demand is an interval average (block or rolling), peak demand is a ratchet the billing reset clears.
  • A reversed CT doesn't stop the meter — it quietly books that phase's energy into the wrong quadrant. The 4-quadrant display is your commissioning truth-teller.

1What class of instrument this is

The SEL-735 is a power quality and revenue meter; the older SEL-734 is a revenue meter. Both are billing-grade instruments: independently verified accuracy classes, sealed-register behavior (TEST mode, billing resets, self-reads), and load-profile output that meter-reading systems like Itron MV-90 ingest directly.

Revenue class vs. relay metering — the honest contrast. The same SEL-735 box states two accuracies: energy registers ±0.06%, but SELOGIC analog values ±3%. A protective relay's MET display is that second kind of number — a percent-class diagnostic built from protection-grade CTs, good for sanity checks and SCADA trending, not for billing. When a relay reading and the revenue meter disagree by a percent, the meter is right. SEL guarantees the 735 holds its ANSI 0.1 / IEC 0.1 S class for at least ten years.

2Four-quadrant fundamentals

Both meters display power flow as delivered (utility → customer) or received (customer → utility), per the SEL-735's IEC VAR sign convention: +W right (delivered), +VAR up (delivered). Each quadrant pairs a watt direction with a VAR direction and a lead/lag character, and the meter keeps separate energy registers per quadrant (e.g. per-phase and 3-phase VARh delivered/received, leading/lagging). Four-quadrant quantities update every 10/12 cycles (10 cycles at 50 Hz, 12 at 60 Hz).

The operating point wanders the P/Q plane and each quadrant feeds its own registers. A reversed CT mirrors the point through the origin — energy books as received while the customer imports. ▶ Animated in the HTML edition.

  • Lead/lag per the 735's table: Q1 lag (IEC PF+), Q2 lead (PF−), Q3 lag (PF−), Q4 lead (PF+). The manual notes other vocabularies exist (import/export/produce per IEC 61557-12) — the SEL display always says delivered/received.
  • Quadrant Device Word bits EQx1–EQx4 (x = A, B, C, 3P) assert while the VAR measurement sits in that quadrant — useful with unsigned VARs and for SER/SELOGIC alarms.
  • Three-phase VARh direction is the per-phase sum: at any instant 3-phase VARh reports only delivered or received (e.g. A del 1 + B del 1 + C rec 4 → QH3_REC = 2, QH3_DEL = 0), even while individual phases differ.
  • Demand vs. energy vs. instantaneous: instantaneous quantities are the live 10/12-cycle values; energy is power integrated every 10/12 cycles into non-rollover billing registers; demand is the interval-averaged quantity of §4.

  • Wiring forms: Form 5 (2-element, 3-wire delta), Form 9 (3-element, 4-wire wye), Form 36 (2½-element, 4-wire wye). Forms 5 and 9 are Blondel-compliant; Form 36 computes VBN = −(VAN+VCN) and is only accurate on balanced voltage — its B-phase PQ, harmonic, and flicker quantities are calculated, not measured. These are transformer-rated meters (external CTs/PTs); CT/PT theory and burden live in CTs & PTs in atlas.
  • Instrument Transformer Compensation (ITC): up to 6 calibration points per phase (EITCI), each with a ratio correction factor (IRCFx_n) and phase-angle-minutes correction (IPAMx_n); the meter linearly interpolates between points and clamps outside them. Test the CT first, then program what you measured — e.g. 0.925 A measured where 1.000 A is ideal → RCF 1.081; 1° lead → 60 minutes. Form 5 has no B-phase CT compensation; Forms 5/36 no B-phase PT compensation.
  • Transformer/line-loss compensation (TLLC): when the billing point isn't the metering point, ETLLC uses nameplate transformer constants and line parameters (positions MPOS/BPOS 1–4) to add or subtract losses from power, energy, and demand. Compensated quantities get uncompensated twins ending in _UC; voltages and currents are never compensated.
  • Watch the near-zero-PF trap: the ANGCUT setting (1–10°) zeroes watts within that angle of 90° (and VARs near 0°/180°) so phase-angle jitter during testing can't smear tiny watt values into both delivered and received. CALCQ picks VECTOR (full-spectrum, harmonics included) or FUND (fundamental-only) VAR calculation.

4Demand metering

Demand type is one global choice, EDEM = THM (thermal), ROL (rolling), or BLOK (block), with interval DMTC ∈ {1, 5, 10, 15, 30, 60} minutes. Demand and peak demand run for currents, per-phase and 3-phase W, VAR (4-quadrant), and VA.

Block vs. rolling windows sweeping the same load curve, while the peak-demand register ratchets up, holds, and is cleared only by a billing reset. ▶ Animated in the HTML edition.

  • Peak demand is a nonvolatile ratchet: the meter stores the maximum with its date/time, overwriting only if the new value is larger, and restores it after a control-power loss. Peak recording is momentarily suspended while the FAULT bit asserts (so a fault doesn't set your billing peak), and FLTBLK blocks peak capture for a set number of minutes after power-up.
  • Reset behavior: resets via QuickSet HMI, the front panel (password-protected access levels), or remotely — global settings RSTDEM/RSTPKDM/RSTENGY take a remote bit (optionally guarded by a remote-analog password, e.g. RB01 AND RA00=12345) so a DNP master can perform the billing reset. The meter records the reset time/date, keeps the previous peak, and counts peak-demand resets.
  • End-of-interval pulse: EOIP asserts at each interval end (EOIPT 1–5 s or OFF); with ROL it fires each subinterval.
  • Predictive demand (ROL/BLOK): projects the present interval's final value each processing cycle; PREDAL asserts after ~3 s above the alarm level — use it to shed load before the peak sets.
  • Reading lag: rolling demand values in the HMI can take up to 2 × DMTC to update — don't call it broken at minute five.

5Energy registers & time-of-use

Energy = power integrated every 10/12 cycles into separate bidirectional and four-quadrant registers (per-phase and 3-phase Wh, VARh, VAh, plus Vh/Ah). Billing energy does not roll over; energy quantities placed in PQ load-profile recorders with end-of-interval function roll at nine dials (999,999,999.99). Energy preload (0–999,999,999,999.999) lets a replacement meter start from the old meter's register values — QuickSet auto-populates the preloads when you read settings from the meter being swapped.

  • TOU programming (QuickSet): a TOU program = Calendar + Season Schedule + Rate Schedules. Rate schedules assign rates A–F across a 24-hour day; you drag them onto day types per season. Calendar actions — season changes, alternate days (holidays), self-reads, demand resets, DST — execute at 00:00.001 on their day.
  • Register structure: a Present Season block (since last season change), a Previous Season block, and 15 pairs of self-read blocks (each pair: season-to-self-read data + the prior season's data). Present-season registers reset on season change or on self-read per the setup option; previous-season registers reset only at season change.
  • Self-reads can also fire on manual peak-demand reset (or a set number of days after one) — the frozen snapshot is what the billing system trusts, and self-reads occur before demand resets when both land together.
  • Total Calculation picks how "Total" registers combine flows: delivered only, received only, del + rec, or del − rec.
  • Getting billing data out: TOU registers are exportable to Excel from QuickSet and readable on any comms port (Modbus map in App. E of the 735 IM); they also serve as front-panel display points.

6Power quality recording (SEL-735)

The 735 is certified to IEC 61000-4-30 Ed. 3 Class A (tested per IEC 62586-1/-2), aggregating measurements over 10/12 cycles, 3 s, 10 min, and 2 h synchronized to real time. Two settings matter for strict Class A: VRMSWIN = ONE (one-cycle rms for sag detection instead of the default half-cycle) and HARMCAL = SUBGROUP (15 Hz harmonic bins instead of 5 Hz components).

7Load profile recording

  • Structure (735): up to 32 recorders × 16 channels. Recorder 1 is the billing recorder: energy always logged as change-over-interval (COI), scaling fixed to primary/kilo (current primary/unity) so Itron MV-90 and similar AMR tools read it without interpretation. Recorders 2–32 are PQ recorders using the global scale settings (PRI_SCA, VOLT_SCA, POWR_SCA, ENRG_SCA).
  • Per-recorder settings: LDFUNC ∈ {EOI, COI, AVG, MAX, MIN}, LDLIST (≤16 quantities), LDAR acquisition rate 3–59 s or 1–120 min (sub-minute rates need Intermediate/Advanced), LMDUR 0.05–7300 days before overwrite. Presets fill recorders for common jobs (IEEE 519, EN 50160, harmonics sets); default recorders 2–6 log the 3 s/10 min/2 h aggregated PQ data.
  • Retrieval: QuickSet HMI (update/chart/export CSV, BIN, TXT, or MV-90 HHF), automatic collection via ACSELERATOR TEAM, or direct MV-90 interrogation. CSV rows carry status bits — DST, power-fail-in-interval, clock set forward/back, skipped interval, TEST-mode data, overwrite.
  • The classic gotcha: changing any LDP recorder setting erases that recorder's data and every recorder after it — add a channel to LDLIST2 and recorders 2–32 clear. Retrieve before you reconfigure. All logs are nonvolatile through power loss.
  • The 734 scales down: 16 channels/1 recorder standard, 192 channels/12 recorders on the 734P; MV-90 always reads its Recorder 1.

8Verification & testing

SEL's documented test philosophy: acceptance (new model), commissioning (verify wiring, rotation, polarity, aux equipment), maintenance (only what hasn't proven itself since last interval). The meter is factory-calibrated; SEL states gain adjustment supplements — never replaces — calibration.

  • TEST mode (touchscreen, pushbutton, or QuickSet): freezes billing data (energy and demand), stops LDP storage, disables normal KYZ outputs and Device Word updates, and accumulates into separate TEST registers displayed in secondary/unity. Test one quantity at a time (Wh/VARh/VAh del/rec, net, lead/lag, Vh, Ah).
  • Pulse outputs for accuracy testing: in TEST mode the KYZDT test pulse drives the front optical port (850 nm) and TEST LED (875 nm) at the watthour constant KET (secondary Wh/pulse) — your test standard's optical pickup counts these against the source. SEL recommends ≥5 pulses at full load, ≥2 at light load, and 5 s of settling before starting.
  • Normal KYZ outputs: four solid-state channels, each with energy quantity KYZDn (default WH3_DEL), constant KEn (0.0001–9999, unit/kilo/mega, primary or secondary), minimum pulse width KYZPWn 25/50/75/100 ms, mapped to OUT401–404. Collect on rising edges or both edges; any fractional-pulse remainder is lost on power loss.
  • Zeroing to a standard: run a Wh test, compute % error (e.g. meter 18.0000 vs. standard 18.0108 → −0.06%), set WGAIN/VARGAIN (±10%) to compensate, then re-verify at a high and a low current point.
  • Self-tests run continuously and assert HALARM (pulsed or latched to a contact) plus status reports — lean on them instead of routine bench trips.

10Field practice & gotchas

  • Rotation and cross-phase checks come free: the meter is its own diagnostic. Commissioning per the manual = verify current/voltage magnitudes, phase rotation (ABC/ACB is a setting — confirm it matches the bus), and contact/input operation from the QuickSet HMI; MET PM (with IRIG) is explicitly called out as a test tool for connections, rotation, and scaling.
  • CT polarity signatures: on a known-import site, every phase should show watts delivered and the expected quadrant bit (EQx1/EQx4). One phase reading watts received while its neighbors deliver — or a 3-phase PF far below the feeder's — is the classic single-reversed-CT signature (§2's mirrored point). Crossed phasing (VA against IB) shows as a ~120° phase error in the instantaneous phasor display.
  • Form 36 fine print: B-phase voltage is synthesized — no B-phase sag/swell/interrupt bits ever assert, and B-phase PQ numbers don't reflect the real system. Single-phase test sets need the 2½-element setup with IB polarity reversed.
  • Frequency reporting can lie when dead: below ~11 V fundamental or >0.9 Hz/s slew the 735 reports nominal frequency, and clamps to the 40–70 Hz limits — a healthy-looking 60.00 on a dead bus is the giveaway.
  • Interval data hygiene: LDP setting changes erase data (§7); TEST-mode intervals are flagged in the export; clock jumps beyond TIME_CHG reset the 10-min/2-h aggregations.
  • Don't "calibrate" in the field: WGAIN/VARGAIN zero the meter to your standard; if you suspect real calibration drift the manual's answer is SEL support, not bigger gains.
