Breaker Failure & Breaker Monitoring

When the breaker doesn't open — BFI, the fast-dropout 50BF, the BF timer race to 86 lockout and transfer trip — plus contact wear, operate-time, and duty-curve monitoring

☰The rules, stated plainly

  • Breaker failure protection is local backup: if the tripped breaker doesn't interrupt, trip everything electrically adjacent — the bus lockout locally, the remote line ends by transfer trip.
  • The scheme is three parts: initiate (BFI) on every trip, supervise with a fast-dropout current detector (50BF), and run a timer (BFPU). Current still flowing when the timer expires = failed breaker.
  • The timer setting is anatomy, not folklore: breaker interrupt time + 50 dropout time + safety margin — and everything after it (86 relay, channel, remote breakers) must still fit inside the system's maximum clearing time.
  • A false BF trip clears a whole bus. Bias the scheme toward security: current supervision beats a 52a contact, which reports the linkage — not the arc.
  • Breaker monitoring is the slow twin: accumulate interrupted current against the manufacturer's duty curve, count operations, and trend operate times so the breaker gets maintained before it becomes the failure.

1The problem: a breaker that fails to interrupt

Zones of protection assume the breaker does its job. When primary protection trips and the breaker fails to interrupt — welded contacts, a broken linkage, a dead trip coil, lost interrupting medium — the fault persists and somebody else has to clear it. The SEL-451 manual frames the choice: remote backup (time-coordinated zones at remote stations) eventually clears it but removes far more equipment; local backup — breaker failure relaying — clears just the electrically adjacent breakers, faster. That usually means tripping the bus lockout (86BF) so every breaker feeding the same bus opens, and transfer-tripping the remote line ends that keep feeding the fault over the line.

The deadline is physical: the absolute maximum fault clearing time set by transient stability and equipment thermal withstand. If a breaker fails, the total time to trip all adjacent breakers must beat that number — which is why every cycle in the BF timer budget is argued over.

2The classic scheme

AG2001-14 builds the whole function from three blocks (there in SELogic on an SEL-311L; native settings on the SEL-451 — both named below):

1 · Initiate — BFIEvery trip of the protected breaker starts BF timing. Internal trip logic, plus optionally an external BFI input paralleled with the trip coil to catch other devices' trips.
→
2 · Supervise — 50BFAn instantaneous overcurrent element proves current is still flowing. Set above max load, below minimum fault. Must drop out fast when current stops.
→
3 · Time — BFPUIf the 50 element is still picked up when the timer expires, declare breaker failure and drive the BF trip output.
  • SEL-451 names: BFI3P1 (initiate SELogic, e.g. := 3PT), 50FP1 (phase fault current pickup, 0.50–50 A sec on 5 A models), BFPU1 (BF time delay, 0–6000 cyc in 0.125-cyc steps). Timer expiry with 50F1 asserted sets FBF1; the trip equation BFTR1 := FBF1 OR NBF1 drives BFTRIP1, which you wire to the 86 lockout. BFULTR1 (e.g. an external input) unlatches the sealed BF trip after the fault clears.
  • AG2001-14 (311L SELogic) names: IN102 = BFI, IN101 = 52a, detectors 50P3/50G3, BF timer SV5PU, retrip timer SV6PU, dropout timers SV5DO/SV6DO := 9 cyc as minimum-duration so the 86 finishes operating before the output opens. Option bits: SV1 52a supervision, SV2 BFI seal-in, SV3/SV4 retrip enabled / unsupervised.
  • Why the 50 must reset fast: after the breaker opens, energy trapped in the CT magnetizing branch keeps a decaying subsidence current flowing in the secondary. A slow-resetting 50 would look like a failed breaker. The SEL-451's open-pole detection logic resets 50F1 in less than one cycle even during subsidence; spec is 1.5-cyc max pickup, 1-cyc max reset. The SEL-352 likewise advertises 50FT elements with fast reset in the presence of subsidence current.
  • Setting 50FP: two philosophies in the 451 manual — set between max load and minimum fault (Iload < 50FP < Imin fault, more secure; the example uses 120% × Iload), or match the line protection's sensitivity (more dependable). Faults involving ground can be covered separately by a residual detector (50RP1) with its own longer timer (NPU1) — a high-resistance ground fault threatens stability less because synchronizing power still flows on the healthy phases.

3The timing race, with real numbers

The 451 manual's Example 1 (25 kV system, 3-cycle breaker) makes the margin anatomy concrete. Work backward from the total clearing time to find the margin, then forward to set the timer:

SymbolWhat it isExample
ttAbsolute maximum fault clearing time (stability/thermal)17 cyc
tlrLine protection max operate time (consumed before BFI)2 cyc
tl-bkLocal breaker max operating time3 cyc
t50r50BF element max dropout/reset time1 cyc
t86Auxiliary (86) lockout relay operate time1 cyc
tchTransfer-trip communications channel time1 cyc
tr-bkRemote/adjacent breaker operating time3 cyc

Safety margin ts = tt − tlr + tl-bk + t50r + t86 + tch + tr-bk… rearranged in the manual to ts = 17 − (2 + 3 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 3) = 6 cycles. Then the timer: BFPU1 = tl-bk + t50r + ts = 3 + 1 + 6 = 10 cycles, with RTPU1 := 4 cyc for the retrip attempt on trip coil 2 along the way.

Both lanes start the same way: trip issued, current flowing, BFPU timing. Healthy: the breaker interrupts at ~3 cycles, 50BF drops within a cycle, and the timer expiry finds nothing — no BF trip. Failed: current is still there at 10 cycles → FBF → 86BF fans out across the bus and DTT clears the remote end. ▶ Animated in the HTML edition.

The margin is squeezed from both sides. Set BFPU too short and a slow-but-healthy breaker plus 50 dropout eats the margin — a false BF trip that clears a whole bus. Set it too long and the total (BFPU + 86 + channel + adjacent breakers) blows through the stability limit. The 451 manual's warning: the operating times of the line relays and the adjacent breakers bound how much margin you can buy.

4On timeout: lockout, retrip, transfer trip

  • Retrip first (if equipped). With two trip coils, attempt a retrip on the second coil before declaring failure — RTPU1 := 4 cyc in the 451 example (RT1 output), SV6PU/SV6T in AG2001-14, instantaneous or time-delayed retrip on the SEL-352. Catches the "trip coil 1 open" failure mode without disturbing anyone else. Put RT1 on a high-current-interrupting output: it has no minimum-duration timer and can open while trip-coil current still flows.
  • 86BF lockout. BFTRIP1 drives the 86, whose contacts trip every breaker on the bus and block reclosing/closing until manually reset. The BF trip output seals in (451: until BFTR1 deasserts, TDUR3D times out, and the BFULTR1 unlatch condition — typically an external input proving the fault cleared — is met). AG2001-14 uses 9-cycle dropout timers for the same reason: hold the output until the 86 has definitely operated.
  • DTT to the remote ends. The remote terminals keep feeding the fault through the failed breaker; they can't see it as their own fault fast enough. AG2001-14 sends the BF status over the existing channel: an 87L transmit bit (T1X := SV5T) or a Mirrored Bits channel (TMB1A := SV5T), with the remote relay including the received bit in its unconditional trip: TR := … + RMB1A. Both channels at once gives redundant transfer trip. Channel mechanics live in Mirrored Bits in atlas.

5Current-supervised vs 52a-supervised

Current supervision (default)

  • The 50BF element is the truth: it measures whether the arc was actually interrupted.
  • Immune to 52a lies — a broken linkage can drive the auxiliary contact while the mains stay closed. The SEL-352 makes this point for its close-failure logic: because it operates on pole current, it "is not subject to misoperation due to mechanical failure" of the breaker or aux contacts.
  • Fast, secure, and self-resetting via subsidence-proof dropout.

52a / low-current supervision

  • Needed when the breaker must trip with little or no current flowing — a weak source behind the breaker, a high-resistance ground fault, or a mechanical/pressure-type device tripping at currents below any safe 50 pickup. No current = 50BF can't see the failure.
  • SEL-451: the no-current/residual scheme (ENCBF1). 50RP1 covers resistive ground faults on its own timer NPU1 (12 cyc in the example, deliberately longer than BFPU1); for true no-current initiations set BFIN1 := 52AA1 so the 52a contact judges success.
  • SEL-352 load-current scheme 2: same as its current-based scheme 1, but adds 52a monitoring for currents below the 50LD element's minimum.
  • AG2001-14: 52a supervision is a settable option (SV1) OR'd with the current detectors.
Rule of thumb from the sources: use current where current exists — it's the only witness to interruption. Use 52a only to cover the cases current can't see, and give those paths their own (usually longer) timer.

6Dedicated BF relays: what the SEL-352 adds

Line and bus relays carry BF logic as a feature; the SEL-352 is a breaker relay — one device per breaker, watching every failure mode the breaker has, not just "failure to interrupt a fault":

Failure modeHow the 352 covers it
Failure to clear a faultFive schemes differing in when the timer starts and what latches. Scheme 2/3/5: timer runs only while trip input + 50FT agree (predictable timing in single-breaker buses). Scheme 1/4: timer starts on the trip input alone — required for ring-bus / breaker-and-a-half, where current split between two breakers makes 50FT assertion timing uncertain until the companion breaker opens. One independent BF relay per breaker in those arrangements.
Failure while tripping loadTwo schemes; scheme 2 adds 52a for current below the sensitive 50LD element.
Failure to closePole disagreement detection — one or two poles didn't make. Operates on pole currents, not auxiliary contacts.
Failure while openFlashover protection (50FO): two schemes — voltage across the breaker + current through it, or single-side voltage + current + the breaker-monitor input. Voltage-nulling logic removes the steady-state voltage difference so only transients register. (The SEL-451 also offers a flashover BF option, EFOBF1.)
Resistors left insertedThermal (I²t) models of trip/close insertion resistors with settable cooling time constants; trips before a cooking resistor fails, and won't reset until the model cools.
Loss of dielectricGas-pressure switch inputs — two interrupter thresholds for alarm / trip / BF trip, plus a CT-dielectric input.

Around the protection: retrip logic, 86BF trip-and-reset logic including automatic breaker isolation — trip the motor-operated disconnect (MOD) once current drops below a threshold, or indicate "safe to disconnect" — point-on-wave controlled close/open, synchronism check, and a recorder: 8 s events at 64 samples/cycle, 512 SER entries, and 512 breaker operation records (type, max current, electrical and mechanical operate times, interrupted energy, per pole).

7Breaker monitoring: catching the failure before it happens

Contact wear against the duty curve

Manufacturers publish maintenance data as permissible close/open operations vs. current interrupted per operation — a log-log curve. Both the SEL-751A and SEL-451 reproduce it from three set points and integrate every interruption against it, per phase (each pole interrupts a different current depending on fault type):

Setting (751A / 451)MeaningExample (SF6, 25 kV class)
COSP1·KASP1 / B1COSP1·B1KASP1Max operations at low current — the mechanical service life; curve is flat left of here10 000 ops at ≤1.2 kA
COSP2·KASP2 / B1COSP2·B1KASP2Midpoint — curve-fit, or set near expected SLG fault current (most interruptions)150 ops at 8 kA
COSP3·KASP3 / B1COSP3·B1KASP3Max interrupting rating; curve falls vertically here12 ops at 20 kA

Constraints: ops 0–65000; kA 0.10–999 (751A) / 1.0–999 (451); KASP3 must be 5–100× KASP1. Above KASP3 the 751A accumulates the excess as KASP3; the 451 jumps that pole's wear straight to 105%. No published curve? The 451 shows how to derive the three points from an I²t rating: COSPn = I²t / (KASPn² · t_arc) — e.g. 750 kA²·s at 1-cycle arcing → 11 250 / 450 / 28 ops at 2 / 10 / 40 kA.

The 751A manual's worked sequence, one phase: 20 interruptions at 7 kA → 10% wear · 290 at 2.5 kA → 25% · 11 at 12 kA → 50% · 3000 at 1.5 kA → 100%, and BCWA asserts. Big currents cost thousands of times more life per operation than small ones — the log curve does the bookkeeping. ▶ Animated in the HTML edition.

  • Initiation: a SELogic equation (BKMON := TRIP on the 751A, BM1TRPA := T3P1 on the 451) triggers the accumulator on its rising edge. The 751A reads the currents 1.5 cycles after the edge, so an instantaneous trip's still-climbing fault current has leveled off before it's recorded. To count trips from other devices too, monitor the trip bus with a spare input (watch trip-lamp pull-up when the breaker is open — set the input pickup ~80% of battery voltage) and OR it in.
  • Counters: the 751A splits accumulated trips and interrupted current into relay-initiated vs. externally initiated, decided by whether the TRIP bit was asserted when BKMON fired. BRE shows it all; BRE W/BRE n P preloads existing wear on an in-service breaker; BRE R resets after maintenance. Data survive power loss and even disabling the monitor.
  • At 100%: 751A asserts BCWA/BCWB/BCWC (and the OR, BCW) — alarm it (OUTxxx = BCW) or drive to lockout on the next trip (79DTL = TRIP AND BCW). The 451 alarms at a settable threshold instead (B1BCWAT → B1BCWAL) and reports wear up to a 150% clip.

Operate-time trends: electrical vs mechanical

The SEL-451 times every operation two ways, and the difference is diagnostic:

Measured from → toAlarm settings (example)What a slow reading means
Mechanicaltrip/close initiation → 52a status changeB1MSTRT := 30 ms trip, B1MSCLT := 70 ms close → B1MSOAL (5 s)Linkage, mechanism, or 52a contact trouble
Electricaltrip/close initiation → open-pole detected / current restoredB1ESTRT := 25 ms, B1ESCLT := 65 ms → B1ESOALArcing-chamber problems — slow arc extinction

Each also has a built-in time-out check: no state change within the threshold + ~100 ms flags a breaker that never moved at all. The 451 keeps a 128-operation history (BRE n H) of mechanical/electrical times and interrupted current — the trend line matters more than any single reading. The SEL-352's breaker records do the same per pole (average + last operate times, interrupted energy), and its BALRM alarm list names the field symptoms directly: slow trip, slow close, 52A contradicts voltage, current while open, trip while open, volts across closed CB.

The rest of the health picture

  • kA per interruption: 451 records each interruption as a percentage of the breaker's max interrupting rating (B1MKAI); exceeding B1KAIAT asserts B1KAIAL — "that one was near the rating, go look at it."
  • Inactivity: B1ITAT (days, e.g. 365) → B1BITAL. A breaker that never operates is a breaker you know nothing about.
  • Mechanism motor run time: B1MRTIN input from the motor contact, alarm B1MRTAT set at measured recharge time + 20% — a spring-charge or air-compressor motor running long is early mechanism trouble.
  • Station DC battery: the tripping energy source. 451: two channels (Vdc1/Vdc2), four thresholds giving five zones (low fail → high fail), AC ripple measurement for charger health, dc ground-fault detection, and the breaker report logs the minimum battery voltage during each operation — a free load test every trip. 751A: under/over-voltage alarms and the dip view during trip/close (needs the enhanced-voltage 5AVI card). SEL-352-3: battery monitoring with four setpoints.

8Gotchas & fine print

  • 751A's built-in BF is minimal by design: BFI := R_TRIG TRIP starts BFD (0.00–2.00 s, default 0.50) supervised by a fixed |I1|+|I2| > 0.02·INOM threshold; BFT trips the upstream breaker. 52ABF := Y adds aux-contact failure detection. Fine for feeder/backup coordination — not a transmission BF scheme.
  • Ring bus / breaker-and-a-half: current division between companion breakers makes 50 element timing unpredictable — pick a scheme that starts timing on the trip input (352 scheme 1 or 4) and give each breaker its own BF relay.
  • BFI dropout/seal-in: the 451 example zeroes BFIDO1/BFISP1 for a single-breaker line (the internal 3PT already has minimum duration via TDUR3D); dual-breaker schemes and external BFI contacts are where those timers earn their keep.
  • Don't let the retrip mask the test: retrip proves coil 2 works, not that BF works. Commission the whole chain — BFI through 86BF through DTT received at the far end.
  • Wear data is per pole. A pole-mounted recloser or breaker clearing mostly SLG faults wears one contact set far faster than the metering-level "operations count" suggests. That's the whole reason the accumulator is per phase.
  • Preload on brownfield installs. A monitor commissioned at 0% on a 30-year breaker is fiction — preload wear (451 accepts up to 100% per pole) before trusting the alarm.
