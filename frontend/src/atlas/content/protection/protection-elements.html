☰The rules, stated plainly

Protection elements

  • An element is a comparison plus a timer: a measured quantity against a pickup setting, then a delay (fixed or curve-shaped) before it asserts a Relay Word bit. Everything else is plumbing.
  • 50 = level + fixed delay. 51 = level + inverse-time curve — more current, faster trip. The time dial scales the whole curve up or down; it does not change its shape.
  • The letter after the number says which current: P phase, N neutral channel, G residual ground (3I0), Q negative sequence (unbalance).
  • An element asserting a bit trips nothing by itself — the bit has to appear in the trip equation (and closing in the close equation). Torque-control settings gate whether an element runs at all.
  • Coordination is stacking curves so the device nearest the fault always wins, with margin.

1How the relay measures: CTs/PTs to phasors

Everything starts as secondary current and voltage from CTs and PTs (1 A or 5 A nominal current inputs on the SEL-751/751A). The relay low-pass filters the analog signal, samples it, and runs a one-cycle cosine digital filter — the combined analog + digital filtering rejects dc offset and all harmonics above the fundamental, leaving clean fundamental phasors. Protection and control logic then process 4 times per power-system cycle (the 751 processes math variables and analog quantities every 25 ms); event reports capture the raw waveform at 16 samples/cycle (751A) or 32 samples/cycle (751), plus 4 samples/cycle filtered.

The SEL-751/751A measurement chain. Frequency tracking works from 15–70 Hz; frequency itself is measured from positive-sequence voltage (needs >10 V, and ≥3 cycles to measure), falling back to positive-sequence current (≥0.1× nominal CT rating), else pinned at FNOM.

CT saturation escape hatch: the cosine filter's magnitude estimate collapses when a CT saturates hard. For phase 50 elements set above 8× the relay input rating (40 A in a 5 A relay), the SEL-751 and 751A also run a bipolar peak detector and switch to it when a harmonic-distortion index says the waveform is badly distorted — so high-set instantaneous elements still trip fast on a saturated CT.

350 vs 51: the two kinds of overcurrent

50 — instantaneous / definite-time

“Is current above X? Then trip after fixed delay D.” Both the SEL-751 and 751A give four levels per current type — e.g. 50P1P–50P4P. Pickup and delay ranges (5 A nom): 751A OFF / 0.50–100.00 A, 50PnD 0.00–5.00 s; 751 OFF / 0.25–100.00 A, 0.00–400.00 s. With delay 0 it operates in <1.5 cycles. Use: high-set close-in fault clearing, breaker failure, blocking schemes.

51 — inverse-time overcurrent

“The further above pickup, the faster the trip.” Pickup 51_P (5 A nom): 751A OFF / 0.50–16.00 A; 751 OFF / 0.25–24.00 A. Curve 51_C (U1–U5, C1–C5; 751 adds IEEE E1–E3), time dial 51_TD, electromechanical-reset choice 51_RS, constant time adder 51_CT (raises the whole curve), minimum response time 51_MR (floor). Use: graded backup for everything downstream.

Every element has a torque control SELOGIC equation — the name survives from electromechanical relays where control current literally produced disk torque. Per the SEL-751 and 751A manuals (same example in both): when 50P1TC := IN401, the 50P1 element is operational only while IN401 is asserted. Directional supervision (§6) is exactly this mechanism.

A 51 element publishes three bits: pickup (51AP — current above pickup, curve timing), trip (51AT — curve timed out), and reset (51AR). ORED51T asserts if any 51 trip bit does.

4The inverse-time curves: U1–U5 and C1–C5

The curve setting picks a shape; the time dial (TD) multiplies it. Operating time is a function of M, the current in multiples of pickup. U1–U3 conform to IEEE C37.112-1996; the equations below are identical in the SEL-751 and 751A manuals. TD ranges: 0.50–15.00 for U.S. curves (both models); IEC: 0.05–1.00 on the 751A, 0.01–1.50 on the 751. Curve timing accuracy (both): ±1.5 cycles plus ±4% between 2 and 30 multiples of pickup. The 751 additionally offers an IEEE family E1–E3 (IEC 60255-151 Types D/E/F — e.g. E1 moderately inverse: TD·(0.1140 + 0.0515/(M0.02−1))) and a 51SAT setting that flattens all curves above M = 30.

  • Steeper = “more inverse.” Extremely inverse (U4/C3) collapses fast at high current — the natural mate for fuses and reclosers, whose melting/clearing curves are also very steep. Moderately inverse (U1/C1) is flatter — timing changes less with fault-current variation, useful where source impedance swings.
  • Electromechanical reset emulation (51_RS := Y): instead of resetting in 1 cycle when current drops below pickup, the element “spins back” like an induction disk on the curve's reset-time equation (e.g. U3: TD·3.88/(1−M²) for M<1). Choose it when coordinating with actual electromechanical relays or reclosers upstream, so both devices ratchet toward trip the same way across repeated fault attempts.
  • Constant time adder 51_CT raises the whole curve by a fixed time; minimum response 51_MR stops it from ever operating faster than a floor.

6Directional supervision — 67 in plain terms

On a radial feeder, current above pickup means a downstream fault. Add a second source, a loop, or DG, and current magnitude alone can't tell which side the fault is on — a plain 51 would trip for faults behind it. A 67 element is just a 50/51 whose torque control comes from direction logic: the relay compares the phase relationship of fault current against a polarizing voltage and declares forward or reverse.

How the SEL-351 (the classic directional feeder relay) does it — and the SEL-751 with voltage inputs implements the same scheme, settings and thresholds included:

  • Phase and negative-sequence direction come from a negative-sequence voltage-polarized element: it computes a negative-sequence impedance and compares it against forward/reverse thresholds derived from the Z2F/Z2R settings (e.g. forward threshold 0.75·Z2F − 0.25·(V2/I2) when Z2F ≤ 0). The results assert Relay Word bits 32QF/32PF (forward) or 32QR/32PR (reverse).
  • Ground direction is chosen by the ORDER setting from up to six polarizing methods — negative-sequence voltage, zero-sequence voltage, current polarization from channel IN, plus low-impedance, Petersen-coil, and ungrounded system variants — with Best Choice Ground Directional logic picking the best available one.
  • Routing: settings DIR1–DIR4 assign forward or reverse to each overcurrent level; all the 51 time-overcurrent elements ride on DIR1. A directional 50 level gets a name like 67P1 with its own delay 67P1D.
  • Loss of potential: lose the PTs and voltage-based direction is meaningless. With ELOP := Y (351) or EFWDLOP := Y (751), the forward logic points assert during LOP — forward-set elements go nondirectional and keep protecting rather than going blind.
Field intuition: a 67 that won't operate during testing is usually a direction problem, not a pickup problem — check polarizing voltage wiring, phase rotation, and whether LOP is asserted, before touching the overcurrent settings.

7Voltage and frequency elements — 27, 59, 81

27 / 59 voltage

  • The SEL-751A gives two levels each of under- and overvoltage, set per-unit: 27P1P/27P2P (OFF, 0.02–1.00 ×Vnm) and 59P1P/59P2P (OFF, 0.02–1.20 ×Vnm). Vnm is per-unit nominal: VNOM for delta PTs, VNOM/1.732 for wye. The SEL-751 gives four levels each, set in volts secondary (OFF, 2.00–300.00 V; wye phase-phase to 520 V), plus phase-phase (27PP/59PP), sync-channel (27S/59S), and inverse-time (27I/59I) variants. Delay on both: 0.00–120.0 s.
  • Sequence variants: 59G (zero-sequence/residual overvoltage — a ground-fault flag on high-impedance-grounded systems) and 59Q (negative-sequence overvoltage), two levels each.
  • Supervise 27 with LOP. Blown PT fuses look exactly like undervoltage. The loss-of-potential logic latches LOP when positive-sequence voltage drops with no corresponding change in sequence currents for 1 s (the 751A manual puts the drop threshold at >25%, the 751 manual at >20%; the 751 also keeps the LOP latch state through power loss). The factory default on both trips undervoltage as (27P1T OR 27P2T) AND NOT LOP.

81 frequency — load shedding

  • Six elements 81D1–81D6, each OFF or 20.00–70.00 Hz (751A) / 15.00–70.00 Hz (751) with a 0.00–400.00 s delay; the 751 adds per-element torque control and optional voltage/current supervision (81VSUP/81ISUP). Set a level below FNOM and it's an underfrequency element; above, overfrequency. Stagger several levels/delays and you have an underfrequency load-shedding scheme in one relay.
  • The relay needs 3 cycles to measure frequency; set delays no shorter than that (the 751A manual recommends ≥5 cycles, the 751 manual ≥3 cycles at the lowest pickup). Frequency comes from positive-sequence voltage (>10 V), else positive-sequence current (≥0.1× nominal CT rating), else it's pinned at FNOM.
  • 81R rate-of-change (four elements, 0.10–15.00 Hz/s, trend INC/DEC/ABS, with voltage/current supervision) sheds load on the slope of decline — faster remedial action than waiting for an absolute threshold. Hysteresis (751A spec): picks up at 100% of setting, drops out at 95%. The 751's measuring window self-adjusts with the pickup: 3 cycles at ≥2.33 Hz/s, stretching to 30 cycles below 0.25 Hz/s.

8Coordination and reclosing — 51 stacking and 79

Making the curves stack

A feeder is a chain: substation relay → line recloser → lateral fuses. Coordination means the device nearest the fault always finishes first, and everything upstream has margin to spare. The levers, in the order you reach for them: pickup (who sees the fault at all), curve family (match the steepness of what's below you — extremely inverse near fuses), time dial (raise your curve clear of the downstream device's total clearing curve), then 51_CT/51_MR for fixed offsets. Verify margin at the maximum fault current at the downstream device, where inverse curves are closest together; keep a deliberate time margin between curves there — and remember the relay's own curve tolerance (±1.5 cycles ± 4% on the SEL-751A) plus breaker interrupting time eat into whatever margin you draw.

The 79 recloser in the relay

Most feeder faults are temporary. The SEL-751/751A reclosing relay gives up to 4 automatic reclosures (751A: E79 := OFF, 1–4; 751: N, 1–4 or C1–C4) and lives in three states — bits 79RS (Reset), 79CY (Cycle), 79LO (Lockout):

  • Close failure: set CFD to the slowest breaker close time plus margin; if the breaker doesn't close in time, CF pulses and drives the reclosing relay to lockout.
  • Sequence coordination (79SEQ) keeps the relay in step with a downstream line recloser: if an overcurrent pickup asserts ≥1.25 cycles and then drops out — no trip, breaker still closed — a downstream device cleared that fault, so the shot counter increments anyway. That lets the relay disable its fast elements in step with the recloser's fast/slow curve sequence instead of overreaching for faults beyond it.
  • 25 sync check supervises closing where both sides can be hot: VP vs VS must both sit inside the healthy-voltage window (25VHI/25VLO) with slip and angle inside limits before 79CLS lets the open-interval close through.

9Beyond the feeder: 21 and 87 in one paragraph each

21 — distance

A distance element divides voltage by current to estimate impedance to the fault. Because line impedance is proportional to length, a reach setting of, say, 80% of the line becomes a fault-location criterion that doesn't depend on source strength the way overcurrent does. Zones with increasing reach and delay replace the coordination stack: zone 1 trips instantly inside the line, zone 2 overreaches with delay as backup. Transmission-line territory — the SEL-351 notably has no phase distance elements; it borrows only the load-encroachment logic (blocking the region of the impedance plane where heavy load lives) from its distance-relay cousins.

87 — differential

A differential element compares current entering a zone against current leaving it — CTs on every boundary. Healthy or through-fault: currents balance. Internal fault: the difference (operate current) is the fault itself, so tripping is fast, sensitive, and needs no coordination with anything outside the zone. Restraint (percentage slope) keeps CT error and saturation from faking a difference during heavy through-faults. That's the whole idea behind transformer (87T), bus (87B), and line-current differential (87L) relays.

10Troubleshooting & fine print

  • Element asserted, no trip? The bit isn't in the trip equation, or torque control is holding it off. Check TR and the _TC settings before doubting the element.
  • 51 timing “wrong” during testing? Check the curve accuracy spec (±1.5 cyc ± 4%), whether 51_CT/51_MR are nonzero, and whether EM reset from a previous shot left the emulated disk part-way travelled.
  • Voltage-based elements misbehaving? Look at LOP first. And on relays without voltage inputs installed, the manual hides these settings entirely — the elements don't exist.
