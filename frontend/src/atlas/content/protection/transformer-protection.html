☰The rules, stated plainly

Protecting a transformer

  • Differential is the primary, but it is not sensitive to everything. Turn-to-turn faults and low-level ground faults near a neutral can produce differential current well below the pickup that inrush security forces on you.
  • That gap is filled by other elements: restricted earth fault for ground faults inside a grounded-wye winding, negative-sequence differential for turn-to-turn faults, and the mechanical devices for anything that generates gas.
  • Backup overcurrent must coordinate with two curves: the downstream devices, and the transformer's own through-fault withstand curve. Both constraints are real, and they sometimes conflict.
  • Inrush is not a fault. Energization draws several times rated current, rich in second harmonic, and every element that only measures magnitude must be secure through it.
  • Overexcitation is a distinct hazard. High V/Hz saturates the core, and the resulting current is rich in fifth harmonic. It needs its own element (24) matched to the equipment's capability, not an overcurrent element.
  • Mechanical protection sees things electrical protection cannot. A 63 or Buchholz operation means gas is being generated inside the tank right now — treat it accordingly.

2Differential, in application terms

The mechanics of percentage restraint, harmonic blocking, TAP settings, and connection compensation are covered in Differential Protection (87). What matters at the application level:

  • Dual-slope percentage restraint with harmonic blocking and restraint is the standard characteristic. Second harmonic secures against inrush; fifth harmonic secures against overexcitation.
  • Winding count drives relay selection. The SEL-787 family supports as many as four windings with dual-slope differential; the SEL-487E covers larger and more complex applications.
  • Compensation is a setting, not wiring. Modern relays undo the delta–wye phase shift and the ratio mismatch in software, replacing the old practice of connecting CTs in delta. Get the compensation setting wrong and healthy load produces differential current — the single most common commissioning failure on a transformer relay.
  • Sensitivity has a floor. Because pickup must sit above the worst-case standing mismatch (tap position, CT error, LTC range), 87T pickup is typically a substantial fraction of rated current. That floor is precisely why REF and 87Q exist.
  • Negative-sequence differential (87Q) is the modern answer for turn-to-turn faults. A shorted turn circulates large current inside the winding while changing the terminal currents only slightly — but it produces negative sequence, which the 87Q element sees.

3Restricted earth fault

REF compares the residual current of a grounded-wye winding's phase CTs against the current measured in the neutral CT. For a ground fault inside the winding, the two disagree; for a fault outside, they match.

AspectDetail
What it needsPhase CTs on the winding terminals and a CT on the neutral bushing or grounding conductor
Why it is sensitiveIt compares two measurements of the same ground current rather than inferring a small difference between large phase currents — so its pickup can be a small fraction of rating
What it catchesGround faults well down toward the neutral end of the winding, which 87T cannot see because the fault current available drops as the fault approaches the neutral
How manyThe SEL-787 family provides as many as three independent REF elements — one per grounded-wye winding
Classic errorNeutral CT polarity. Reversed, the element operates for external ground faults and stays quiet for internal ones — the worst possible outcome
Confirm which CT you have. A CT on the neutral bushing measures only the current in that winding's neutral. A CT in the grounding conductor below a point where other grounds connect measures something else. The three-line drawing settles it; the drawing being wrong is common enough that a primary injection test is worth the effort.

4Backup overcurrent and the damage curve

Overcurrent on both windings serves two purposes: backup if the differential is out of service or fails, and protection for faults just outside the differential zone that no other device clears quickly. Setting it means satisfying three constraints at once.

Three constraints, one curve. The differential covers the zone; the 51 exists for everything the differential does not, and it lives in whatever gap the other two leave. (Animated in the browser.)

5Volts per hertz (24)

Core flux is proportional to voltage divided by frequency. Push V/Hz above the design value and the core saturates, magnetizing current rises steeply and distorts, and stray flux heats structural steel and windings. It is a thermal failure mode with a time–magnitude characteristic, not an instantaneous one.

  • When it happens: generator step-up transformers during startup and shutdown (excitation applied at low speed), load rejection (voltage rises while frequency has not yet fallen), and voltage regulator failures.
  • How it is set: an inverse-time characteristic matched to the transformer's or generator's overexcitation capability curve, usually with a definite-time high-set stage.
  • Availability: on the SEL-787 family, volts/hertz protection with frequency tracking from 15 to 70 Hz is offered on the models with voltage inputs, which is what makes it usable for generator step-up and variable-frequency applications.
  • Why not just use overvoltage: at reduced frequency, normal voltage is already overexcitation. A 59 element set for rated frequency will not protect a machine coming up to speed.

7Commissioning

  1. Verify the nameplate against the settings: MVA, voltages, connection, and the actual DETC position. A two-tap discrepancy is a standing differential of several percent.
  2. Prove every CT ratio and polarity individually, and confirm the neutral CT separately — REF depends entirely on it.
  3. Check the compensation settings against the vector group. QuickSet's phasor display is the practical tool here.
  4. Inject to prove restraint and slope, including a through-current case that should not operate.
  5. Energize and read the standing differential under load. This is the acceptance test that catches everything the bench missed. It should be a small percentage; anything larger is real.
  6. Exercise the LTC across its range and watch the differential. It should move slightly and stay well inside the slope characteristic.
  7. Prove each mechanical trip path by operating the device or its test lever — not by jumpering the relay input, which proves only that the relay works.
  8. Record the through-fault counter's starting value so the accumulation means something later.

8Gotchas

  • Compensation applied twice. CTs wired in delta and compensation set in the relay cancels the shift once too often. On a retrofit, check the physical CT connections before trusting the old drawings.
  • LTC range not accounted for in the slope. A transformer at the end of its tap range produces a real, permanent differential. Slope 1 must accommodate the full range plus CT error.
  • 50P on the HV set too low. If it can see LV-side faults it will trip for feeder faults the feeder relay was going to clear — a coordination failure that only appears during real faults.
  • REF neutral CT reversed. Operates for external ground faults, blind to internal ones. Prove it with primary injection through the neutral if at all possible.
  • Sudden pressure misoperating on through-faults. Supervise it (current check or short qualification time) rather than disconnecting it.
  • Backup 51 that violates the damage curve at high current. Check the curve at the maximum through-fault, not just at pickup — the two curves converge at the right-hand end of the plot.
  • Volts/hertz omitted on a generator step-up. The transformer is exposed during every startup and every load rejection, and no other element covers it.
  • The relay's own voltage inputs unused. Models with voltage inputs unlock 24, 27/59, 81, and synchronism check — on the SEL-787-3S the VS/VBAT channel can serve either as a sync-check voltage or as a station battery monitor. Deciding this at ordering time is cheaper than discovering it later.
