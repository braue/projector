☰The rules, stated plainly

Grounding decides your ground protection

  • The grounding method sets Z0, and Z0 sets ground-fault current. Nothing else about ground protection can be decided until you know how the source neutral is treated.
  • You are trading two things against each other: fault current magnitude (damage, arc-flash energy, and step/touch potential) against voltage rise on the unfaulted phases (insulation stress and equipment rating).
  • Solidly grounded gives large, easily detected ground current and holds unfaulted phases near normal. Ungrounded gives almost no current but drives the healthy phases to full line-to-line voltage.
  • Low current means voltage-based detection. Below roughly a few tens of amperes, current elements stop being reliable and 3V0 (59N) becomes the primary indicator.
  • Ungrounded and high-resistance systems can run with one ground fault on — that is the point — but a second ground on another phase becomes a line-to-line fault through two long paths.
  • A delta source has no neutral. If you need one, you create it with a grounding transformer (zig-zag or wye–delta), and that transformer then becomes a zero-sequence source that your protection has to account for.

2The neutral shift — why ungrounded systems are detected by voltage

On an ungrounded system, a solid ground on phase A does not push much current anywhere — there is no low-impedance return. What it does is move the reference: phase A is now at ground potential, and B and C sit at full line-to-line voltage above ground. The voltage triangle keeps its shape and slides.

The generator does not care; the insulation does. Sustained 1.73× stress on the healthy phases is what eventually finds the next weak spot. (Animated in the browser.)

Restriking arcs and transient overvoltage. On an ungrounded system a low-current arcing ground can extinguish and restrike each half cycle, pumping charge onto the system capacitance. The resulting transient overvoltage can reach several times normal and puncture insulation far from the original fault. This is the strongest practical argument against leaving genuinely ungrounded systems in service, and the reason high-resistance grounding — which damps the oscillation — is usually preferred over pure ungrounded operation.

3“Effectively grounded” and why the ratio matters

The industry definition is a ratio test on the sequence impedances at the point in question: a system is effectively grounded when X0/X1 ≤ 3 and R0/X1 ≤ 1. Meeting it keeps the coefficient of grounding at or below about 80%, which means healthy-phase voltage during a ground fault stays under roughly 1.4 × VLN.

  • Why you care: surge arrester ratings are chosen for the expected temporary overvoltage. Arresters selected for an effectively grounded system will fail on a system that is not.
  • It is a local property. Opening a source breaker can leave a section ungrounded even though the system is nominally effectively grounded — a real risk when a delta–wye transformer's wye is the only ground source for a feeder and the transformer trips.
  • DER makes this current. An inverter or a generator that can island a section must not leave that section ungrounded, which is why interconnection standards care so much about the transformer connection at the point of interconnection. See DER Interconnection.

4Creating a neutral: grounding transformers

Delta sources have no neutral to ground. When a ground reference is required — to bound overvoltage, to give ground relays something to measure, or to satisfy an interconnection requirement — you add one.

DeviceHow it worksNotes
Zig-zag (interconnected wye)Two half-windings per leg wound on different cores in opposition: high impedance to positive and negative sequence, low impedance to zero sequenceCompact and purpose-built. Presents essentially only a zero-sequence path, so it does not supply phase-fault current
Wye–delta grounding bankGrounded wye on the system, closed delta providing the circulating pathThe delta must be closed and rated; often the same unit serves station service
Resistor or reactor in the created neutralLimits the current the grounding transformer will passTurns a solid ground reference into low- or high-resistance grounding
A grounding transformer is a zero-sequence source, and protection must treat it as one. It contributes ground-fault current to faults anywhere on its side of the system, it needs its own protection (a neutral 51N is common, coordinated with its short-time thermal rating), and it changes the zero-sequence network your fault study is built on. SEL application guidance covers protecting zig-zag grounding banks specifically (AG2002-20).

Sensitive ground elements need a real neutral CT. Below roughly 5–10% of the phase CT rating, residual current computed from three phase CTs is dominated by CT error. If the design calls for sensitive ground detection, specify a window (flux-summing) CT that encircles all three conductors — it measures the true residual with one core and one error term. SEL application guidance on sensitive ground directional elements (AG99-02, AG2009-17) assumes this arrangement.

6Gotchas

  • Do not assume the grounding method — verify it. Look at the transformer nameplate connection, look for an NGR cubicle, and read the fault study's Z0. Sites are frequently mis-documented, and retrofits change grounding without updating drawings.
  • An NGR is short-time rated (10 s and 60 s ratings are common). Protection must clear well inside that rating, and a resistor that has been through several long faults may already be damaged — check continuity as part of commissioning.
  • High-resistance grounded systems that were set to trip defeat their own purpose, and the reverse is worse: a low-resistance system set only to alarm will burn the resistor.
  • Multiple ground sources on one bus split the ground current. Two grounded-wye transformers in parallel each supply part of it, so a neutral element on one may see only half of what the study's total suggests.
  • Ferroresonance is a genuine hazard on ungrounded and lightly loaded systems with wye-connected PTs — single-phase switching can excite a nonlinear resonance that destroys the PTs. Loading resistors on broken-delta windings are the usual mitigation.
  • Grounding changes when a breaker opens. Ask what the system looks like in every switching configuration, not just the normal one, and check that no configuration leaves an energized section ungrounded.
