Voltage Regulation

The step voltage regulator and its brain, the SEL-2431 — setpoint, bandwidth, time delay, line-drop compensation, and reverse power on DG-rich feeders

1Why feeders need regulators

Load current through a feeder's series impedance drops voltage — a feeder that leaves the substation healthy can serve low voltage miles out, and the drop grows and shrinks with load all day. A step voltage regulator is a tap-changing autotransformer in the line that boosts or bucks in small steps to hold delivered voltage inside an acceptable band. The SEL-2431 manual's framing: the control regulates system voltage to an "in band" region; too high or low ("out of band") and it commands a tap change to bring it back. Typical installs: three single-phase regulators per substation feeder position (one SEL-2431 each), plus regulators out on the feeder. The band itself is a setting on a 120 V secondary base — §3.

2The machine: a 32-step regulator

The SEL-2431 is configured for use with standard single-phase, 32-tap step-type voltage regulators. The mechanism, as the manual walks through it:

  • Series + shunt winding. A tapped series winding either adds to (boost) or subtracts from (buck) the line voltage; polarity is chosen by a motor-driven Raise/Lower reversing switch.
  • Taps −16 … N … +16. Eight stationary contacts plus bridging positions give 16 steps each side of Neutral. At tap +16 the entire series winding boosts; at −16 it bucks; at N (neutral) the series winding is completely bypassed — source and load are at the same voltage. The reversing switch flips as the regulator passes through neutral.
  • Step size. Global setting VADJUST defaults to 0.100 pu — a nominal 10% full-raise regulator — so one tap is 0.100/16 = 0.00625 pu, the classic 0.625% (5/8%) step (0.75 V on the 120 V base). Some Type B designs need e.g. VADJUST = 0.111.
  • Type A / Type B are the two ANSI regulator configurations (setting TYPE); Type A is "straight," shunt winding on the S-terminal side.
  • Compatibility. One control retrofits many makes: Cooper/McGraw Edison, GE, Howard Industries, Siemens/Allis, Toshiba CR-3, ITB RAV-2, Romagnole SVR (interface ordering option + wiring-harness retrofit kits). Line-to-neutral or line-to-line (open/closed-delta) with a properly rated regulator.

3The control loop: setpoint, bandwidth, time delay

Three settings decide every automatic tap. Voltage is regulated on the 120 V secondary base:

F_TD1Time Delay 1 — out-of-band must persist this long before the first tap1.0–999.0 s60.0

Band edges are F_CNBND ± F_BNDWD/2 — with defaults, 119–121 V. Above 121 V the HBNDFWD bit asserts; below 119 V, LBNDFWD; between them, IN_BNDF. Bandwidth exists to prevent continual hunting. A mirror setting family (R_CNBND, R_BNDWD, R_TD1…) serves reverse-direction regulation.

The classic loop: load rises → regulated point drifts below band → F_TD1 qualifies the excursion → first raise (0.75 V) → still out, TD2 spaces a second raise → in band. Load falls and the same happens in reverse. ▶ Animated in the HTML edition; dashed lines show the light/heavy-load envelope.

Four reset characteristics for the first-tap timer (F_CHAR / R_CHAR)

What happens to a partially-run F_TD1 timer when voltage pops back in band is selectable — F_CHAR: FAST, DISC, DELAY, DLY_FRZ (default DISC):

  • FAST — resets immediately on re-entering the band (like "sequential" mode on other controls). Weakness the manual calls out: short in-band excursions can repeat indefinitely and a mostly-out-of-band voltage never gets a tap.
  • DISC — unwinds like an induction-disc relay at F_DISC seconds per elapsed second (range 0.10–2.00, default 0.60; typical 0.60–1.10 to coordinate with legacy controls). Resets immediately if voltage jumps to the opposite band or power flow reverses.
  • DELAY / DLY_FRZ — hold, then reset after F_DLYRS (0.0–999.0 s).

4Line-drop compensation: regulating a point you can't measure

Without LDC the control holds its own terminals flat and the feeder end under-serves at heavy load. LDC makes the control mimic the line: from local voltage and current it calculates the voltage at a downstream regulation point (an important load center) and regulates that instead. The compensated voltage VCMP is a calculated value visible in metering; the regulator moves its real output VL however much is needed to hold VCMP at F_CNBND.

Making the settings (forward direction)

Convert the primary line impedance from regulator to regulation point into secondary volts at rated CT current (0.2 A secondary):

  • VLDCFWR = Rpri × (BASE_SEC/BASE_PRI) × CTPRIM  ·  VLDCFWX = Xpri × (BASE_SEC/BASE_PRI) × CTPRIM (each −24.0 to +24.0 V sec)
  • Multiply both by 1.73 for line-to-line (open/closed-delta) connections; by 2 on a single-phase two-wire circuit; by 1.67 if one of the two wires is a multigrounded neutral.
  • Conductor changes / tapped load: model the line section as its majority conductor type, then lower R and X somewhat (keeping the R/X ratio) since not all load flows to the section end — the manual says some experimentation may be needed.

Simplified alternative when feeder power factor is steady: set LDC to a straight slope — output rises from F_CNBND at no load to a chosen full-load voltage. Near unity pf: VLDCFWR = (CTPRIM/IFL)×(VFL − F_CNBND), VLDCFWX = 0.

Same load swing, two policies. Local-only regulation pins VL and lets the end of the feeder sag out of band; LDC computes the remote voltage from local V, I and the R/X settings and raises VL just enough to hold that point flat. ▶ Animated in the HTML edition.

  • LDC limits. VLDCFWMP/VLDCFWMN (and RV twins; OFF, ±0.1–24 V sec) cap how far LDC may push VL up (positive max — the traditional case where |VCMP| ≤ |VL|) or down (negative max — advanced applications). These limit the relative VCMP–VL difference; VMAX/VMIN (§6) limit absolutes.
  • LDC off (ELDC := N, or SETLDC0 := Y while voltage reduction is active) → VCMP = VL directly.
  • Utilities with their own LDC setting sheets: voltage-base conversion is already inside the SEL-2431 — don't double-apply it.

5Reverse power and operating modes

Power-flow direction is determined from current and the normal load-side voltage. Exactly one of three Device Word bits is asserted: FORWARD, REVERSE, or NO_TORK ("no torque" — too little signal, or a nearly pure capacitive/inductive load). Refined forward/reverse regions use current thresholds 50FWDP/50REVP (0.002–0.020 A sec, i.e. 1–10% of rating). The Group setting OPMODE then decides which direction gets regulated:

OPMODEScenario (per the manual)
LOCKFWD / LOCKREVFlow expected always forward (normal load-side) / always reverse.
IDLEREVAlways forward, but no regulation when flow is indeterminate (e.g. no load).
BIDIRFlow varies (multiple system interfaces); regulate VL on forward flow, VS on reverse.
COGENFlow varies due to DERs on the load side; during reverse flow voltage is still regulated from the forward perspective (at the load-side terminal) — the utility source is still the stiff bus. This unique region uses the reverse LDC settings.
FLEXDGFlow direction proves nothing (DG + multiple sources); regulation direction is determined from the voltage-regulation effectiveness of issued taps.
Why direction matters: the manual's Figure 4.5/4.6 walk-through — if the stiff utility source ends up on the "load" side (a feeder reconfiguration can do this) while the control keeps regulating VL, every tap moves VS instead, in the undesired direction. The control asks for raise after raise and drives the far-side voltage down. Current direction alone cannot detect this when DG is interspersed.
  • BIASMODE (OFF/FWD/REV, for BIDIR and COGEN) picks a regulation direction inside the small-current zone (|I| below both 50FWDP/50REVP), so a lightly loaded regulator — e.g. few customers between it and a normally-open switch — isn't "stranded" unable to tap.
  • FLEXDG mechanics: after each tap the control measures tap-delta voltage DLT_VLS (change in VL per tap; full value = VADJUST/16 × BASE_SEC ≈ 750 mV sec at defaults). VL moves ≥ TAPDELTH (dflt 0.60 pu of full) → taps control VL, run cogeneration-style; VL moves ≤ TAPDELTL (dflt 0.50 pu) → taps aren't controlling VL, regulate VS regardless of current direction. Works even with only source-side voltage connected (monitors DLT_VSS).
  • Inhibit/block: SELOGIC INHIBSV plus mode "no-operation" zones assert INHIBIT — no automatic tapping even in AUTO. A BLOCKSV equation blocks tapping; current firmware resumes TD2 timing/tapping after a block clears if still out of band.

DA tie-in: feeder reconfiguration (see the DAC pages in this section) is exactly what flips source direction mid-day on a regulator — the COGEN/BIDIR/FLEXDG discussion above is the regulator-side half of that story.

6Voltage limits, runback, and voltage reduction

VMAX / VMIN — first-tier limits (automatic mode only)

With ENLIMIT := Y: VMAX (OFF, 108.5–145.0 V sec) and VMIN (OFF, 80.0–131.5; not settable within 3 V of VMAX) protect customers near the regulator from LDC pushing VL too far while chasing VCMP. Priority: VL (a real measurement) stays in range; VCMP (calculated) is allowed out of its band. Automatic tapping is suspended when VL comes within 1 V of the limit. If VMAX is exceeded, further raises are prevented but lowers are not — subsequent lowers spaced by TD2 continue until VL is back under.

Runback — second-tier, actively taps back

Beyond VMAX + DBNDH or VMIN − DBNDL (deadbands OFF/0–6 V), timer RUNBKPU (0–9999 s) qualifies the excursion, then the control instigates tapping toward normal; after the first tap, timing reverts to TD2. VOLTAGE HIGH/LOW LIMIT LEDs flash at 0.5 Hz. Documented triggers: a large load change (this feeder or a sibling on the same transformer), cold-load pickup after an outage, switching manual→auto at a bad tap, or a group change with different limits.

Voltage reduction — deliberate demand relief

Lowers the center band (and both edges) by a set percentage when demand exceeds supply — the same system voltage is suddenly "high," and the regulator taps down. Three stages, each OFF or 0.1–10.0% (VREDFP1–3 fwd, VREDRP1–3 rev), time-qualified by VRSPU1–3. Modes (VREDMOD): LATCH (legacy SCADA binary-coded inputs), PULSE (legacy momentary-pulse advance), CONTROL (SELOGIC-driven — e.g. staged on load-current elements 50L1–3). VRIPROG asserts and the VOLTAGE REDUCTION LED lights while active; SETLDC0 := Y zeros LDC during reduction; OVRIDSV typically overrides in LOCAL/MANUAL. Gotcha: stage-to-stage transitions leave a gap with no reduction for the next stage's qualification time.

Software tap limits: TAPMAX (OFF, 8–16) / TAPMIN (OFF, −8 to −16) constrain automatic range; MAXTAP/MINTAP bits assert at the bounds.

7The SEL-2431 box itself

Metering & accuracy

  • Real-time V, I, frequency, power; 32 samples/cycle, control processing once per cycle, full-cycle cosine filter, 10-cycle averaging. Voltage control accuracy ±0.3% measured (±1.0% calculated), −40° to +85°C.
  • Max/Min with date/time stamps, separate fwd/rev registers. Thermal demand + peak demand (fwd/rev current, power, 4-quadrant Q); DMTC = 5/10/15/30/60 min, dflt 15. Energy with separate in/out real and reactive registers. Harmonics 2nd–15th plus THD — also usable in control logic.
  • IEEE C37.118 PMU output to 60 msg/s (Level 1 with PHCOMP = Y) — the DS suggests using it to identify the connected phase of downstream regulators.

Controls & logic

  • 4 setting groups, each with separate forward/reverse set points, delays, LDC, mode, reduction, limiting, runback — switchable by command or SELOGIC (calendar/clock, overcurrent, demand…).
  • AUTO/MANUAL and REMOTE/LOCAL pushbuttons with SELOGIC overrides (REMOTSV/LOCALSV/AUTOSV/MANULSV — force or block either mode). Factory default LOCALSV := MANUAL: selecting MANUAL forces LOCAL, and REMOTE can't be selected until back in AUTO.
  • 7 programmable pushbuttons/LEDs, 2×20 LCD, 32 display points; motor-control section as pushbuttons or toggle switch; voltmeter test terminals auto-follow the present load-side PT with power-flow direction (customizable via VTJ_MODE).

Communications & SCADA

  • Front EIA-232 standard; optional rear serial cards (EIA-232, 2/4-wire EIA-485, multimode fiber) and dual-Ethernet card in Port 2. IRIG-B in via Port 1 serial cards. Front Type A USB port takes a flash drive for settings transfer and automatic report backup.
  • Serial: SEL ASCII + Compressed ASCII, Fast Meter, Fast Operate, DNP3 Level 2 slave (rear ports), C37.118. Ethernet: DNP3, C37.118, SEL protocols over Telnet (everything but ping disabled until SET P E).
  • DNP3: six sessions combined serial+Ethernet (max 4 Ethernet; 4 DNP sessions on Ethernet kills Telnet engineering access). Polled report-by-exception is SEL's recommended access method. Event buffers 1024 binary / 100 analog / 32 counter; SER-assigned binaries carry true occurrence time stamps, others get ~1 s map-scan time. Custom maps via SET DNP with per-point class, deadband, scaling. Notable: binary outputs like BO_F_TD1 bump the active F_TD1 in +5 s steps — on-the-fly settings shifts over DNP; the DS calls this "Distribution Automation Ready." Protocol mechanics: see the DNP3 pages in atlas.

Security

  • Access Levels 0 → 1 → 2 (settings/control) plus restricted Level C; Level 2 password required for e.g. TAP W. Shipped with default passwords — SEL's warning: change them at installation or accept unauthorized-access risk. Appendix I documents interfaces, access controls, and logging.

8Tap tracking, operation counters, and wear data

Tap changes are the regulator's wear currency — the same way breaker ops are for a recloser. The SEL-2431 tracks position and counts everything, in nonvolatile memory:

  • Position feedback: the control watches the operations-counter switch (Siemens: closed on odd taps; Howard: closed on even; GE/Toshiba/ITB/Romagnole: momentary pulse per tap) or Cooper's holding switch, plus raise/lower/hold motor current (MRAISE/MLOWER/MHOLD), and steps its internal tap counter. No feedback? TPFDBK := N dead-reckons TAPDUR seconds after each pulse.
  • Counters: Total (NV, updated every operation), Year-to-date, Month-to-date, Sliding-24-hour, Since-Reset (TAP R), and per-tap counters for every position −16…N…+16 (0–32767 each) — the histogram shows where the regulator lives and whether it's misapplied. TAP shows the report; TAP W preloads totals (0–500000) from a replaced control on retrofit.
  • Signal (Load) Profile Recorder: up to 16 analogs (e.g. IL, VLSEC, VCMPSEC, TAP_POS) every 5/10/15/30/60 min (SPAR; dflt 15 quantities @ 15 min), 8640 NV entries, oldest overwritten — ≥90 days even at 16 analogs/15 min. SELOGIC SPEN gates recording. Read with PRO, or CPR for spreadsheet-ready compressed ASCII; changing the list clears stored data.
  • Event reports & SER: oscillographic events include control element response, regulator interface signals, and motor current; the SER time-stamps state changes. All nonvolatile — valuable at sites with no comms yet.

9Field notes & gotchas

  • Verify neutral with the NEUTRAL POSITION LED only. The manual is explicit: tap position 0 equals neutral, "but only the NEUTRAL POSITION indicator should be used for verification of neutral position." That LED is driven by the regulator's own neutral switch, is 120 Vac powered, and can operate even when the control is not enabled — the check that matters before any bypass switching of the regulator. NO_NEUT asserts if Tap = 0 but the neutral switch isn't closed (the counter is lying).
  • Check regulation direction before trusting AUTO on any circuit that can back-feed: wrong direction means each tap moves the stiff-source side "in the undesired direction" and the control hunts (§5). The REVERSE POWER LED and the OPMODE/BIASMODE tables are the map.
  • Tap-position drift alarms: TAP_OFF (counter-switch state disagrees with odd/even tap — Siemens/Howard), TAP_DIF (neutral switch closed but tap didn't arrive from ±1), OPNOCUR (counter pulsed, no motor current). Whenever the neutral switch newly closes, the internal tap resets to 0. RSELIM/LWRLIM (no tap, no motor current after TAPDUR) usually means the regulator is at its raise/lower limit switch. Abnormal bits route to the ALARM output in factory defaults.
  • Make-specific timing: Siemens needs NEUTOVR (extra motor run at neutral so the reversing switch lands closed — too long and AUTO overshoots neutral; may need reducing as the regulator ages; also raises the TD2 floor to 1.3 s + NEUTOVR). TAPDUR must exceed one tap time but stay under two (Cooper Quik-Drive: just shorter than its ~250 ms tap). GE counter switches drift out of alignment — GE_WIND ignores extra pulses; some old McGraw-Edison units assert neutral with DHR — TAP_DHR := Y suppresses false re-syncs.
  • Delta installs: global settings CONFIG/DELTA/D_LAG/D_LEAD handle the angle work for open/closed-delta banks — but you still apply the 1.73 multiplier to the LDC R/X settings yourself (§4).
  • Retrofit hygiene: preload the old control's operation count with TAP W (zeros for unknown per-tap history) so wear records survive the swap; and change the default passwords.
