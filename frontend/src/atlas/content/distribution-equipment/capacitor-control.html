☰The rules, stated plainly

Capacitor banks

  • A shunt capacitor bank is an economical generator of reactive power. It corrects power factor, supports voltage, reduces losses, and lets the network carry more real power.
  • The failure mode is progressive. One capacitor element fails, the remaining elements in that group take more voltage, and the next failure comes faster. Unbalance protection exists to catch it before the cascade.
  • The IEEE limits are the settings basis. Continuous operation is permitted up to 110% of rated RMS voltage, 180% of rated RMS current including harmonics, and 135% of rated reactive power. Those numbers, not intuition, bound your alarm and trip levels.
  • Energizing a bank is a transient event. Inrush is limited by series impedance or by point-on-wave switching, not by hoping.
  • A capacitor stays charged after it opens. Units rated above 600 V must have an internal discharge device that brings residual voltage to 50 V or less in five minutes — which is why the reclose block after opening is measured in minutes, not seconds.
  • Control strategy is a system decision. Voltage, VAr, time, temperature, or a coordinated volt/VAr scheme — each answers a different question.

1What the bank is for

Reactive power must be supplied somewhere. Supplying it from a capacitor bank near the load, rather than from generation at the far end of the network, is dramatically cheaper: it frees line and transformer capacity, cuts I²R losses, and raises local voltage. The arithmetic is on the AC Power Basics page — at 0.85 power factor, the same 10 MW load draws 18% more current than at unity.

2How a bank is built, and how it fails

Alarm on the first unit lost so it can be replaced during planned work; trip before the remaining units exceed their rating. (Animated in the browser.)

Fusing

  • Externally fused — a fuse on each unit. A failed unit is isolated and visibly identifiable, and the remaining units carry on at elevated voltage.
  • Internally fused — fuses on individual elements inside the can. Failures are less disruptive but not visible from outside; only unbalance measurement finds them.
  • Fuseless — series strings designed so a failed element short-circuits and the string continues with slightly higher voltage on the rest. Relies entirely on unbalance protection.
  • Individual capacitor fuses also handle the discharge current from parallel units into a failed one — a large, fast transient that the fuse must clear.

Unbalance sensing — the element that matters most

  • Voltage differential (87V) compares the voltage across part of the bank against a reference tap. Because it looks at the bank's internal division of voltage rather than at the system, it is immune to system unbalance — which is the central problem with current-based schemes on distribution systems that are never perfectly balanced.
  • Neutral current or neutral voltage unbalance is the alternative: measure the current in the neutral of a grounded bank, or the voltage across the neutral of an ungrounded one. Simpler, but it responds to system unbalance and to inherent bank tolerance as well as to failed units.
  • Two stages. Alarm at the loss of the first unit; trip before the survivors exceed the 110% continuous voltage limit. The number of units per group determines how much margin exists between those two points.
  • Inherent unbalance must be compensated. Manufacturing tolerance means a healthy bank shows a small standing unbalance. Measure it at commissioning and set the compensation, or you lose most of your sensitivity to the first failed unit.
Do not set unbalance protection from a formula alone. Measure the actual standing unbalance on the energized, healthy bank and compare it against the calculation. A large discrepancy means either a unit is already gone, the compensation is wrong, or the bank is not built the way the drawing says.

4Switching transients

Energizing a capacitor into a bus is closing a switch onto an initially uncharged capacitance. The current inrush is limited only by the source impedance and any deliberate series impedance, and the voltage transient propagates.

  • Inrush magnitude and frequency can be very high, especially back-to-back — energizing one bank while a parallel bank is already in service, where the already-charged bank supplies a very fast, very large current into the new one.
  • Mitigation: a switched or fixed impedance in series with the bank (an inrush reactor), or point-on-wave switching control that closes each pole at the instant its voltage is near zero.
  • Voltage magnification downstream is the customer-facing consequence: the switching transient can be amplified at a lower-voltage capacitor elsewhere on the system, tripping drives and sensitive loads.
  • Restrike during opening is the switch's problem, and it is why capacitor switching duty is a specific switch rating rather than a general one.
  • Reclose blocking. A bank that has just opened is charged. Units rated above 600 V must discharge to 50 V or less within five minutes via an internal discharge device — so the control must block reclosing for at least that long. Closing onto a charged bank produces a transient far worse than normal energization.

Every hunting cycle is a switch operation, and switch mechanisms have a finite number of them. (Animated in the browser.)

Every automatic strategy needs guard rails. A voltage override that opens the bank on overvoltage regardless of the primary strategy; a minimum time between operations to protect the switch; a daily operation limit; and a defined behavior when communications fail. Switch mechanisms have a finite number of operations, and a control that hunts will consume them in months.

6Integration

  • Status points: bank position per stage, control mode (auto/manual/local/remote), unbalance alarm and trip, blown-fuse indication where available, switch operation counter.
  • Analogs: bus voltage, bank current per phase, neutral or unbalance quantity, reactive power, and the number of operations today.
  • Controls: open and close per stage, with feedback from the position status — never from the fact that a command was sent. See SCADA Integration.
  • The unbalance quantity is worth trending as an analog even when it is nowhere near alarm. A slow drift is a unit degrading, and it gives you months of warning.
  • Coordinate with the regulator. A capacitor bank and a voltage regulator both controlling the same feeder voltage will fight unless their setpoints, bandwidths, and delays are designed together — see Voltage Regulator Control.
  • Where a programmable controller drives the scheme, an SEL-2411 Programmable Automation Controller is a common choice: ruggedized to industrial and utility standards, −40 to +85 °C, a fast 4 ms logic loop, 1 ms-accurate sequential events recording, and the usual protocol set (Modbus RTU and TCP, DNP3 serial and LAN/WAN, Mirrored Bits, SEL ASCII and binary, IEC 61850) with PRP and RSTP.

7Gotchas

  • Unbalance protection left at calculated settings. Measure the standing unbalance on the healthy bank and compensate, or the first failed unit will be invisible.
  • Reclose block too short. The discharge device needs its five minutes. Closing early is a violent event.
  • Overcorrection at light load. A fixed bank sized for peak raises voltage above limits overnight. Size fixed banks for minimum load and switch the rest.
  • Harmonic resonance. A capacitor bank and the source inductance form a resonant circuit. If that resonance lands near a harmonic the load produces, currents can be amplified severely — and the 180% current limit is reached with a perfectly normal fundamental. Detuning reactors are the usual answer.
  • Hunting against the regulator. Two controllers, one voltage, no coordination. Widen bandwidths and stagger delays.
  • Blown fuse not reported. A visibly blown fuse on a pole-top bank can sit for a year. Unbalance alarming into SCADA is what closes that gap.
  • Switch duty ignored. Capacitor switching is a specific duty. A general-purpose switch will fail early, and it will fail while closing.
  • Treating a series capacitor like a shunt one. Series compensation changes line impedance and can invert the apparent direction seen by distance elements — an entirely different protection problem.
