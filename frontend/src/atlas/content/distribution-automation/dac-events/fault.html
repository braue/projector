Permanent faults

Detection, lockout confirmation, locating the fault, immediate isolation, extended isolation, miscoordination, close-onto-fault, and protection/breaker failure.

§07DA Lib / DA Adapter 105.0.2Code-derived

The rules, stated plainly

  1. A fault is only an event when a device that saw it is open or locked out. A fault indication with everything still closed is just latched and waits.
  2. A fault is permanent only when a device in the fault path reports reclose lockout (or the feeder also has a source event). A successful reclose never confirms.
  3. The fault is located on the section below the deepest device that saw fault current and above the first device downstream that did not.
  4. The switches bounding that section are opened at confirmation time, before the restoration solve, if they can break load and isolation is allowed.
  5. Uncontrollable closed devices do not bound the zone; the zone grows to the next controllable switch.
  6. If an upstream device locked out while a downstream device also saw the fault, that is miscoordination; what happens next depends on EnableMiscoordinationDetection and on whether the downstream device has protection.

At the device

  • Fault.Raw = Fault OR FaultAB OR FaultBA. A rising edge creates Fault.New; the condition is held (Conditioned) until processed or reset. Equipment/Fault/EQUIPMENT_FAULT_DETECTOR
  • After FaultHoldTime (120 s) the DAC sends a fault-target RESET to the relay, but only if the feeder is not locked out and not in UPDATE.
  • Adapter: with LatchFaultAtDevice FALSE the adapter latches the relay's fault bits itself for 2 minutes (cleared by the DAC's RESET output). Fault = Fault | Fault_A/B/C | Grnd_Trip; FaultFwd/FaultRev map to AB/BA according to the forward direction setting. ADP/DA_REC.prv_StatusInputs
  • Reclose in progress is inferred: the device opened within the last 10 s with a fault present. It ends on lockout, or when the device has been closed again for 2 × EventDetectTime. An open device with reclose in progress is treated as tripped and locked out for fault-location purposes.
  • Adapter lockout = lockout AND open (all phases in three-phase mode). In SwitchMode, open itself counts as lockout.

Detection on the feeder

Each scan, class_Fault.Detect walks from the feeder source through equipment with filtered continuity. A new fault on any device latches the feeder's Fault.Latched (only if armed). TripNew fires once when a latched fault coincides with a device that is open or locked out; that is the event trigger. Out-of-service devices are skipped. On permitted-loop devices only the fault direction pointing into the current search direction counts. Feeder/Event/Fault/class_Fault.Detect

Lockout confirmation

class_Lockout.Confirm searches the feeder for a device that saw the fault, has reclosing, and is in lockout. Finding one sets LockoutLatched and polls the feeder again for fresh data. A source event on the feeder counts as lockout. If a directional device at the first switch level (the feeder breaker zone) shows the fault in the reverse direction, the fault is behind the breaker: Fault.OffFeeder is set and the feeder leaves ANALYZE without acting. Feeder/Event/Fault/class_Lockout

The fault is confirmed only when lockout is latched AND every device on the feeder has answered the follow-up poll (mFEEDER_CheckDeviceResponses).

Locating the fault

class_Fault.Confirm walks downstream carrying what has been seen upstream (fault, trip, lockout, protection, controllable). At each device: Feeder/Event/Fault/class_Fault.prv_Confirm / prv_ProcessFault

  • A device that saw fault while nothing downstream of it saw fault marks the fault location: the node below it is Faulted, and every piece of equipment downstream up to the next fault-detecting device is flagged Faulted.
  • Faults located this way stay "unresolved" until an upstream lockout is found; the feeder cannot leave ANALYZE while any are unresolved (it times out instead).
  • When the lockout device is processed, each unresolved fault location is handed to the extended-isolation search.

Isolation and extended isolation

fb_PermFaultIsolation.FindIsolation searches outward from each fault node. Feeder/Event/Fault/ExtendedIsolation/fb_PermFaultIsolation

  • It passes freely through non-isolating equipment (lines, loads, buses, capacitors, regulators) and through closed switching devices that cannot be controlled (no switch, inhibited, abnormal, protection failure). The zone therefore extends to the next device the DAC can actually open.
  • Each controllable switching device reached is an isolation point: SW.Isolation, shed-permissive set, source-group matching cleared. It is opened immediately if: it is closed, not a connector, and either load-break-capable or already disconnected from every source; AND the feeder allows isolation; AND there is no open-phase or loss-of-source event on the feeder (unless that feeder isolates open phases).
  • If the far side of an isolation point is energised and moving away from the source, it becomes a root (load to restore) when the feeder allows restoration, otherwise a non-restorable root.
  • Bus-fault rules: if the faulted zone contains a bus/line object configured with AdjacentBusIsolate or AdjacentLineIsolate (set with a device FB's SetLoadBus method), the search extends one more switch into the adjacent bus or line zones. Loads in that extension are marked ExtendedFaultZone and reported. Isolation points beyond the first switch carry the ExtendedIsolation flag (device output).
Interpretation "Open immediately" happens during ANALYZE, before the solver has even run. That is why isolation switches can be seen opening, then a pause while the solve completes, then the tie closing. Non-load-break switches are never opened while still energised; they wait until the upstream device has de-energised them.

Miscoordination

Miscoordination = an upstream device locked out while a device downstream of it also saw the fault. The feeder's Miscoordination output sets when the downstream device has protection and at least one non-connector device saw the fault. The handling: Feeder/Event/Fault/class_Fault.prv_ProcessFault

CaseWhat the DAC does
EnableMiscoordinationDetection ON (Adapter default) and the downstream device is controllableCorrects it: the downstream fault-bounding switches isolate the fault; the locked-out upstream device is added to the ReturnClose list so it is reclosed to restore the healthy section between them. Shed permissives and restore flags are set below it.
Detection OFF, downstream device has no protection (e.g. a switch with fault indication) and is controllableSame correction. This is ordinary sectionalising: the upstream protective device is expected to trip for faults below a non-protective switch.
Detection OFF, downstream device has protectionNo correction. Equipment between the locked-out device and the fault is flagged MiscoordinationIsolation and treated as non-restorable. Only load beyond the fault isolation is restored.
Locked-out device is at the first switch level and the feeder has a source eventNever reclosed (the source side is not trustworthy).
Locked-out device is inhibited (cannot be reclosed)An isolation search is run from it instead. If it is a pseudo breaker (no current and no voltage measurement) at the first level, the whole breaker zone is isolated.

If a ReturnClose device is also on the isolation list, the solver drops the reclose and reports it as a miscoordination switch.

Close onto fault

Every close command opens a 10 s window. Inside it, the DAC declares CloseOnFault on the device if: the device's feeder sees a new trip; or a new fault arrives and the device is open 10 s later; or the fault input stays up through the close and the device then opens. Equipment/EQUIPMENT

  • CloseOnFault is latched, makes the device abnormal, blocks further CLOSE outputs, aborts the running sequence (DA_SEQ.CloseOnFault → abort, AbnormalCondition), and is cleared only by a feeder reset or system reset.
  • A "possible close on fault" (inside the window) already blocks repeat closes.

Protection failure and breaker failure

Devices have a ProtectionFailure input (Adapter: BreakerFailure/ProtectionFailure point). fb_ProtFailure arms on a rising fault at that device, provided the neighbouring feeders are armed and not abnormal and the device itself is not abnormal; it disarms when the event finishes, when the device is commanded open, or after FaultHoldTime with nothing in progress. Equipment/ProtFail/fb_ProtFailure Feeder/Event/Fault/ExtendedIsolation/fb_BreakerFailure

  • Protection failure while armed (IsolateFailedDevice): during fault confirmation the DAC isolates around the failed device itself, at the next controllable switches, with ExtendedIsolation. Bus-terminal rules (BusTerminalIsolateLine/Bus) extend this one zone further from a bus.
  • Protection failure while not armed: the device is simply abnormal.
  • A device with protection failure never counts as controllable, so it is never chosen as an isolation point.
  • Adapter substation logic: any feeder breaker on a bus reporting breaker failure raises a source event on every feeder of that bus (§09).

Temporary faults and other non-events

  • Reclose succeeds: no lockout, so the fault never confirms; the feeder sits in ANALYZE until AnalyzeTimeLimit, then clears. Nothing is switched. The fault targets reset after FaultHoldTime.
  • Fault indication with nothing open: latched, no trip, no event.
  • Reverse fault at the breaker zone: OffFeeder, no action (bus or adjacent-feeder fault).
  • Both directional elements on a device asserting together: the device becomes abnormal (bad fault indication).
