DAC — Permanent Fault & Miscoordination

How the faulted zone is located, what miscoordination detection changes, and the four conditions an alternate feed must meet

always enableddeepest FI + upstream LOreclosing goes first4 restoration criteria

☰Core facts

  • Permanent fault detection is always enabled and cannot be disabled.
  • The deepest fault indication names the zone. An upstream lockout confirms it is permanent. Two signals, two jobs.
  • Devices between the deepest fault indication and the head of the feeder are not required to report fault indications. This supports coordination where a deep fault is below the pickup of upstream devices.
  • No lockout, no permanent fault. The sequence returns to Idle.
  • With miscoordination detection on, the deepest fault indication still names the zone, even when it is not the device that locked out.

1Locating the zone

Detection runs in the Analyze step, on fault current indications and lockout indications from devices on the feeder. The feeder is evaluated zone by zone, starting at the point farthest from the source.

The fault indication farthest from the source identifies the affected zone. ▶ Animated in the HTML edition: the current, the flags, the lockout, then the search.

Once the zone is identified, the DAC waits for a lockout indication before calling the fault permanent. This gives local reclosers and breakers their chance to clear a temporary fault. If reclosing succeeds, no lockout arrives and the sequence returns to Idle.

If the fault is permanent, an upstream breaker or recloser clears it and goes to Lockout. With correct coordination that is the device immediately upstream of the faulted zone.

2The fault indication bit

Zone location is only as good as the fault indications. Requirements:

  • Assert on fault current pickup, not on operation — 51-element timing. This allows miscoordination to be corrected, and lets non-fault-interrupting and non-load-break switches contribute.
  • Do not assert on ground overcurrent alone unless a protection operation occurred because of it. An upstream single-phase fault unbalances the remaining phases downstream. Use the neutral element's operate, or supervise it by relay operation.
  • Do not assert on inrush or cold-load pickup. A 12-cycle restrain on pickup filters inrush; torque control of the fault pickups handles cold load.
  • Latched and momentarily-asserted indications are both supported, provided DNP events are configured so the assertion is reported. Latching is a per-device setting.
Recommended SELogic shape (UDG): latch the fault trigger, feed it through a variable timer with a 120 s pickup-to-dropout so the status self-resets after about two minutes, and expose a remote bit the DAC can use to reset it early.
A fault indication anywhere on the feeder suppresses open-phase and dead-bus evaluation entirely. See Open Phase & Loss of Source.

3Miscoordination

The device that locks out is not always the one immediately upstream of the fault. Miscoordination detection is an option. With it enabled, the DAC uses the most downstream fault indication to locate the event, regardless of which device locked out.

CB A over-tripped. The deepest fault indication (R1) still names the zone correctly.

Multiple lockouts. On the first lockout indication the DAC forces an update poll of every device on the event feeder, so all lockouts are seen and considered. The zone is still located by the deepest fault indication.

Effect on isolation

Miscoordination detectionSwitches opened to isolate
OffAll switches downstream of the affected zone.
OnAll switches downstream, plus the switch immediately upstream of the affected zone, commanded open if it did not already operate on protection. The over-tripped device upstream can then be closed back in.
Non-fault-interrupting devices produce this pattern normally. A fault downstream of a switch that cannot interrupt gives a fault indication at the switch and a trip to lockout at the next upstream device — the definition of a miscoordination. The DAC operates accordingly, but reports these as permanent fault events that did not miscoordinate, because protection behaved as intended. The switching order matches miscoordination correction; the logs do not say so.

4Restoration

An alternative feeder is available only if all four hold:

1 · No abnormal conditionsNothing abnormal anywhere on the alternative feeder. See Abnormal Conditions.
2 · Not excludedThe Exclude input of its DA_CIRCUIT is not asserted.
3 · Live everywhereAll voltage sensing on the alternative feeder shows live indication.
4 · Enough marginEnough unused capacity for the load served on the zone to be restored.
Field note — restoration does not care whether the zone being restored carries load. Restoration targets zones, not amperage. A de-energized unfaulted zone measuring 0 A is restored like any other. Zone load enters only through criterion 4, as the capacity the alternate must cover — so a zero-load zone satisfies it trivially.

Restoration comes from an alternative feed across a normally-open tie, or — in the miscoordination case — from the normal source, by closing the over-tripped device back in. Both occur in the same solution.

If one alternate cannot take everything, the DAC evaluates sectionalizing solutions, splitting the de-energized zones across several alternates. Worked example on Reconfiguration & Switching.

Settings groups are evaluated against the new topology and changed where an association exists — see Settings Group Changes.
