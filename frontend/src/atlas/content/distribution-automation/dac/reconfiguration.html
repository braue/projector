DAC — Reconfiguration & Switching

The scheme-wide solver — switch eligibility, scenario ranking in order, and the rules governing every open and close

☰Core facts

  • Scenarios are generated fewest-operations-first. A solution that re-energizes all affected load with everything under normal limits ends the search.
  • Eligibility differs by role: Bypass disqualifies a sectionalizing switch, Out of Service disqualifies a restoration switch, InhibitControl disqualifies both.
  • Any scenario that permanently parallels two sources, or uses an unavailable source, is rejected.
  • Execution order is always opens → settings groups → closes.
  • Closed transition requires matching source groups.
  • If the Open of a closed-transition pair fails, the DAC re-opens the switch it just closed.

1Idle

Scheme-wide, not per feeder. It monitors for confirmed events and confirmed RTN commands from any feeder, and moves to Analyze when one or more feeders have reported a confirmation and all feeders are in the Idle step of their own sequences.

Concurrent events across several feeders are therefore solved as one problem.

2Analyze — switch roles

The three roles. One device can be a candidate for more than one role across scenarios.

RoleQualifying devicesDisqualified by
Isolation Identified in the event sequence and reported in. All switches adjacent to a permanent fault. All switches adjacent to an open phase if the DAC is set to isolate open-phase conditions. —
Sectionalizing All closed switching devices in the affected network: every closed switch downstream of a permanent fault or open phase, and every closed switch in an overloaded feeder. Bypass or InhibitControl
Restoration All open switching devices: every open switch downstream of a permanent fault or open phase, and every open switch adjacent to an overloaded feeder. OutOfService or InhibitControl

Isolation switches appear in every scenario the solver builds.

Transfer depth

TransferDepth on DA_MANAGER_AR sets how many layers of neighbouring feeders are considered.

0No restoration. No feeders are considered.
1Tie points of the local feeder to its neighbours only.
2 — recommendedLocal ties plus the ties of one layer of neighbours. Allows rebalancing that frees capacity for stranded load.

Higher depth increases solve time. Set 2 except where the number of switches involved is small.

3Scenario generation and ranking

Scenarios open and close combinations of eligible switches, generated and evaluated fewest-operations-first. Each is checked for validity, then ranked.

Rejected

  • Scenarios involving unavailable sources.
  • Scenarios that permanently connect multiple sources in parallel.
  • With load shedding disabled: scenarios that shed energized zones the triggering event did not de-energize.

Ranked, in this order

  1. Minimize overload above temporary limits.
  2. Minimize load left de-energized.
  3. Minimize overload above normal limits.
  4. Minimize the maximum load restored by any one feeder above Level 1 but below Level 2 voltage.
  5. Minimize the total load restored by feeders above Level 1 but below Level 2 voltage.
  6. Minimize switching operations.
  7. Minimize the maximum load restored by any one nonpreferred source.
  8. Minimize the total load restored by nonpreferred sources.
  9. Bias toward the normal source, if no overload above normal limits results.
  10. Minimize the worst-case capacity margin below normal limits.
Nonpreferred source = a DA_CONNECTOR, or a circuit fed by a DA_BUS with events in progress on a neighbouring DAC. The DAC will sectionalize more than capacity alone requires to avoid using one.
Voltage levels. Level 1 is live but sagging; Level 2 is fit to restore from. AllowSelectLevel1 = FALSE — a Level 1 feeder cannot be chosen. AllowRestoreLevel1 = FALSE — it can be chosen, but restoration waits for Level 2. See Loading, Capacity & Voltage.
Early exit. Beyond criterion 6 the solver already favours fewer operations by generating simple scenarios first. When it finds a solution that re-energizes all affected load with every component below its normal capacity limit, it selects that solution and evaluates no more complex scenario. If no such solution exists it continues and takes the best available — which may be partial restoration or no action.

When it cannot restore everything

The CapacityLimit alarm is set on DA_MANAGER_AR and on the DA_CIRCUIT of the original event feeder. The alarm gives no reason — only that the system lacks capacity to respond completely.

For an RTN command, no complete solution means the sequence aborts to Idle and sets CapacityLimit. RTN is all-or-nothing — see Return to Normal.

4Reconfiguration step

The solution is a list of switches to open, switches to close, and settings groups to confirm. The step fails if it exceeds ReconfigurationTimeLimit (default 120 s) and returns to Idle.

Failure causeAlarm on DA_MANAGER_AR and the event feeder's DA_CIRCUIT
An abnormal condition appearedAbnormalCondition
A commanded device did not respond correctlyCommandFailure
Insufficient voltageVoltageLimit

Switches to open

  • Isolation switches open immediately.
  • Switches to open that are downstream of an isolation switch open immediately. This covers some or all sectionalizing switches.
  • Switches to open for load transfer — not downstream of an isolation switch:
    • closed transition not enabled → open immediately;
    • closed transition enabled → stay closed until the sourcing and receiving feeders are temporarily paralleled.

The DAC waits for status indication from each device confirming the operation.

Settings groups

Before any close, the DAC determines which feeder each switching device on the impacted feeders will be fed from after reconfiguration, compares that against the configured associations and the device's current group, and sends the change where they differ. It waits for confirmation from every such device before proceeding. See Settings Group Changes.

Switches to close

Open transition — the DAC closes a switch when all of these hold:

  • No continuity from this switch to any switch-to-open that has not yet opened.
  • Only one source connected, on one side.
  • No switching operation in progress on either side.
  • Live voltage on at least one side.

Closed transition — the DAC closes if the above hold, or if all of these hold:

  • Two sources connected, one each side, with the same phase relationship and same SourceGroup.
  • No switches-to-open on one side, and one or none on the other.
  • No switching operation in progress on either side.
  • Live voltage on at least one side.
Closed-transition pairing. The DAC identifies the switch-to-open paired with the switch-to-close and sends the Open as soon as the closed status is detected from the switch it closed — not on a timer. If that Open fails, the DAC sends an Open to the switch it just closed. Command failure here is declared using UpdateTimeLimit.

Once the solution is executed the sequence returns to Idle.

CloseDelay (default 0 s) delays a close command between queueing and sending. Set it non-zero on high-speed comms where isolation steps report opened before the mechanism has finished moving.

5Worked example — sectionalizing across two alternates

Fault between CB A and R1 on Circuit A. CB A trips to lockout. Three zones of 100 A each are de-energized and unfaulted — 300 A. Circuit B has 250 A margin, Circuit C has 150 A. Neither can take 300 A.

The execution below, played out: fault, lockout, isolate, then each alternate picking up what it can carry. ▶ Animated in the HTML edition.

R1 is the isolation switch, R3 the sectionalizing switch, R4 and R5 the restoration switches. Execution:

  1. Open R1 and R3 simultaneously.
  2. R3 confirmed open → close R5. 100 A to Circuit C.
  3. R3 and R1 both confirmed open → close R4. 200 A to Circuit B.
  4. R4 and R5 confirmed closed → sequence complete. 300 A restored, no capacity limit exceeded.

R5 closes as soon as its own precondition is met, without waiting for R1. The close rule is per-switch continuity to unopened switches-to-open, not a global barrier.
