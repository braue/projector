DAC — Device Behavior Modifiers

Device properties that change what the DAC will do — load-break capability, single-phase lockout, non-interrupting switches, unseen breakers, spares

☰Core facts

  • All reclosers are load-break capable. Only switches can be marked otherwise.
  • A load-break incapable device is never opened while connected to a known source. It becomes eligible when its surroundings are disconnected from every known source.
  • Default is prefer-closed: a device with mixed phase statuses is modeled as closed. Only a configured single-phase-lockout device uses prefer-open.
  • Every switching command the DAC sends is three-phase, in every configuration and for every event.
  • Fault-interrupting capability is not required for inclusion. Remote control and fault reporting are.

1Load-break incapable switches

Set per switch (Load Break Capable = False). Reclosers have no such setting.

The DAC will never open a device that is not rated load-break capable while it is connected to a known source. It will consider the device eligible if the surrounding area is disconnected from all known sources. This applies to three-phase energization and to single- or two-pole energization from single-phase operate devices.
Usable forAnswer

The three-step sequence

Where the event will drop the customers anyway — open-phase isolation downstream of such a device — the DAC will consider:

1 · Isolate widerOpen a load-break capable device further upstream, de-energizing the section.
→
2 · Open the incapable deviceNow unloaded, it can be operated.
→
3 · Close backRe-close the upstream device to restore the non-event zone.
A sequence requiring extra switching purely to de-energize such a device, open it, and re-energize it is not considered. Example: a normal open point that is not load-break capable and is presently closed. The DAC will not perform the RTN, even if open-transition operations are allowed.

2Single-phase operate devices

Lockout Mode on each recloser:

Gang LockoutThe device always performs ganged lockout.
Single-Phase LockoutThe device is eligible to lock out individual phases.
DynamicThe device changes between both modes. The Dynamic Single-Phase Lockout Mode data point must be mapped so the DAC can read the current mode.

Prefer-closed and prefer-open

Default — prefer-closed. A device with a discrepancy in its individual phase statuses is modeled as closed, because it can energize downstream load. The DAC generates switching decisions using three-phase behavior only and will not attempt individual-phase commands.

Configured as single-phase lockout — prefer-open during idle times. A single or two phases tripping to lockout results in the system considering the device opened and locked out, which allows AR operations to start.

Commands are confirmed against all three phases. When the DAC sends a control to a single-phase operating device, it confirms all three phases align with the command before considering it successful.

Response behavior

  • In response to a single- or two-phase event the DAC sends only three-phase open or close commands. If the initiating event is a miscoordination, the correction produces full three-phase isolation of the faulted zone even though the protection operation was single- or two-phase.
  • Despite prefer-open, the system tracks partially energized zones to prevent damage to load-break incapable switches.
  • Pole status discrepancy is ignored for a device configured for single-phase operation. On a ganged device it is logged to the SOE and is not an abnormal condition.

RTN first step

Once a complete return sequence is determined, the DAC's first operation is to three-phase close every device with one or two phases open, putting the system into the prefer-closed model the RTN plan was built against. The normal RTN sequence follows.

Exception at a connector. A partially energized zone at a DA_DAC_INTERCONNECT location may have its energized phases temporarily dropped during an RTN, even with closed-transition-only RTN configured. Configure the devices bordering an interconnection point for gang lockout.
Field note — single-phase lockout behaves like an open phase without isolate: it opens the next downstream recloser and restores from the alternate.

3Non-fault-interrupting devices

Any device may be included as long as it has remote control and fault reporting. Motor-operated switches qualify. Reclosers may be configured to act as switches while in specific settings groups.

TermMeaning
Recloser / breakerDetects fault current and can interrupt it.
SwitchDetects fault current, cannot interrupt it.
Switch ModeA device that can interrupt but is currently configured not to. Model as a recloser and map the Switch Mode status.
Their presence changes the reporting, not the algorithm. A fault downstream of a non-interrupting switch produces a fault indication there and a trip to lockout at the next upstream device — the signature of a miscoordination. The DAC operates accordingly, including operating the non-interrupting device, but reports these as permanent fault events that did not miscoordinate.

Miscoordination handling is automatically enabled for any non-fault-breaking switches, regardless of the scheme-level miscoordination setting.

4Breakers the DAC cannot see

Virtual breakers

A feeder breaker modeled electrically with no DNP session — Data Source set to the reserved keyword [Virtual]. Every feeder must originate with a feeder breaker. The Lockout Period setting defines how long the area downstream must be de-energized before the DAC considers the device locked out. Ignored for physical breakers.

Predictive breaker modeling — pseudo breakers

DA_PSEUDO_BKR predicts a substation breaker's position, overcurrent pickup and lockout status from the first downstream device:

  1. The breaker is assumed closed unless one of the following applies.
  2. It is assumed to have a fault indication asserted if the first downstream device reports a fault indication.
  3. It is assumed open if it has a fault indication and the first downstream device reports dead source-facing voltage.
  4. It is assumed locked out if the condition in rule 3 persists for the configured breaker lockout period.

Purpose: support miscoordination between the breaker and the first downstream device. Without it, protective overreach from a non-communicating device produces simultaneous fault indications plus loss of source voltage, which results in no operation from the DAC.

Do not combine with shot-counting schemes. With sectionalizers or similar devices driven to lockout during a breaker open period prior to lockout, the DAC may perform additional isolation from the assumed breaker-open status.

5Spare breakers

For substation transfer-bus arrangements. Each breaker may specify one other breaker as its spare, and one device may be the spare for multiple primaries. An operator control replaces the primary device with the spare in the topology model, so AR continues when a primary is out of service.

  • While a spare is swapped in, the feeder's operational targets attach to the spare's indications.
  • The system supports one spare swapped in for multiple primaries simultaneously.
Feeder breakers modeled purely as spares still count toward the feeder count used for licensing.
