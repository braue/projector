DAC — Open Phase & Loss of Source

Broken jumpers, dead buses and lost sources — detection prerequisites, the fault-current block, and the two isolation modes

☰Core facts

  • A fault current indication anywhere on the feeder stops open-phase evaluation. Voltage may be dragged down by the fault, so the DAC will not read it.
  • Detection searches from the substation outward. Fault location searches from the far end inward.
  • The DAC must first have seen healthy voltage on every PT on the feeder before it will call an open phase.
  • Open-phase detection is always delayed (default 60 s) for upstream reclosing and external switching. Open-phase sequences run longer than permanent-fault sequences.
  • Feed mode opens one switch, and only if it can restore what is beyond it. Isolate mode opens every bordering switch unconditionally.
Field note — open phase with isolate is essentially a fault. Open phase without isolate opens the next downstream recloser and restores from the alternate. Single-phase lockout behaves like open phase without isolate.

1What counts as an open phase

Broken jumpers, hanging conductors, high-impedance opens, and dead-bus conditions. A loss of substation voltage, such as from a transmission outage, is treated as an open-phase condition.

Live/dead status comes from analog voltage values compared against percentages of nominal, or from binary live/dead statuses computed in the end device. Either or both may be used, with no change to the underlying behavior. The indication must be non-latching.

2Prerequisites, in order

1 · Healthy historyAll PTs on the feeder must have shown healthy voltage for either half the open-phase confirmation time or the full event detect time, whichever is shorter. Half the 60 s default = 30 s.
→
2 · No fault currentA fault indication anywhere on the feeder blocks evaluation for open phase and dead bus.
→
3 · Sustained indicationVoltage indications must show a possible open phase for EventDetectTime (default 10 s) before the event sequence starts.
→
4 · Confirmation delayOpenPhaseConfirmTime, default 60 s, inside the Analyze step.
A permanent open-phase condition that triggers a fault indication at inception but never drives reclosing to lockout produces no DAC operation. The fault indication blocks open-phase logic; no lockout ever confirms a fault.

AnalyzeTimeLimit must exceed OpenPhaseConfirmTime, or open-phase detection cannot complete before Analyze times out.

3Locating the zone

With no fault indications present, the feeder is evaluated zone by zone starting at the substation feeder breaker. When a measurement reports dead, every measurement point downstream is checked to confirm it is also dead. The first device reading dead while its upstream neighbour reads live bounds the affected zone.

Substation dead bus

Same logic, same suppression by fault current. When every point including the feeder breaker reads dead, the zone upstream of the substation breaker is the affected zone.

Live below dead

Live voltage reported downstream of dead indications for an extended period is treated as a voltage discrepancy. The DAC ceases monitoring for open-phase conditions on that feeder until the issue resolves and healthy voltages are reported on all PTs for half the open-phase confirmation time. See Abnormal Conditions.

Incomplete PT coverage

Where the device bounding the downstream edge of a zone is closed and has no three-phase voltage sensing, an open phase upstream of it is indistinguishable from one downstream. The DAC merges the two zones into a single extended zone.

In the extended zone the PT-less device is bypassed in the electrical model for locating the event. It is still monitored for abnormal conditions. The removal carries into the isolation step — the isolation zone becomes the extended zone, so isolation occurs at devices further out than the one-line suggests.

4Isolation — the two modes

Isolate Open Phase DISABLED — "Feed Open Phase"

For feeders where most load is single-phase and a zone with an open phase can still safely feed customers.

The DAC evaluates whether any zones downstream of the break could be restored by a three-phase source:

  • Alternate available → open the switch immediately downstream of the affected zone, de-energizing the downstream zones for restoration from the alternate.
  • No alternate available → do not open. Energized load stays on one or two phases.

If the affected zone branches to several downstream paths, the analysis runs per branch.

Isolate Open Phase ENABLED

For feeders where three-phase loads are common.

The DAC identifies every device bordering the affected zone and opens all of them, upstream and downstream, regardless of whether any alternative feed exists. Branching zones are fully isolated.

The zone is cut out on both sides, then whatever is beyond it is restored if possible.

Devices opened this way carry an Opened to Isolate Open Phase target, cleared by a scheme or feeder Reset.

5Restoration

Identical to the permanent-fault case. An alternative feeder is available only if: no abnormal conditions; not excluded; all voltage sensing shows live; enough unused capacity for the load picked up. Insufficient capacity on a single alternate sends the solver to sectionalizing solutions.

Settings groups are evaluated against the resulting topology — see Settings Group Changes.

6Loss of source

The whole-feeder case of the same voltage logic: loss of three-phase voltage at all modeled devices of a feeder, from a substation or transmission event.

AspectBehavior
TriggerAll PTs along the feeder report dead voltage for a user-specified time.
OutcomeOpen the feeder breaker, or the most upstream device excluding bus or transformer mains. Restore all sections from alternates.
EnablingEnabled automatically whenever open-phase detection is enabled. Otherwise EnableLossOfSourceDetection (default TRUE).
Without the substation modeled, loss of voltage at the breakers is always read as a loss-of-source event. With main breaker status and bank/bus lockout status monitored, the DAC distinguishes a manual operation (no AR) from a protection operation (AR).

The SourceEvent input is the explicit way to report an upstream event — see Source Events & Underfrequency.
