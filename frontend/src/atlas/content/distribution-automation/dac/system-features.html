☰Support caveats

Several features are not supported in the DAC Extension and must be configured in library code — marked below. Some are additionally advanced-users format: no front end, limited or no built-in logging. SEL's position is that these should be implemented only with in-depth assistance from SEL Engineering Services.

None of these features cause system events. They change how reconfiguration calculates, or how switching is carried out.

1Alternate Feed Service recloser not in Extension advanced

Prevents sudden extreme overloads when an external system switches a large customer load onto a feeder the DAC does not monitor. A dedicated recloser sits on the feed to the AFS customer; the customer's system decides when to transfer onto it.

ConditionDAC action
Feeder not in its normal configuration, no load flowing through the AFS recloser, feeder capacity insufficient for the anticipated customer loadOpen the AFS recloser.
Available feeder capacity sufficient for the anticipated customer load, and voltage above the Level 1 threshold at all measurement locationsClose the AFS recloser.

The comparison is margin against the load the external transfer switch could move. Temporary margin for the open decision, normal margin for the close decision. The DAC can operate the AFS recloser only if its feeder is Armed with no abnormal conditions.

Automatic operation is suspended when

  • An event is being processed on the connected feeder. The DAC will not close. Automatic operation resumes after the event.
  • The recloser is open and fault current has been detected. Manual close required to return to automatic operation.
  • The recloser is open when an abnormal condition clears from it. Manual close required.

2Automatic transfer switch not in Extension advanced

Modeled as two switches with a load between them, typically one open and one closed. Automatic/Manual refers to the local control mode: in Automatic the local transfer logic may operate the switch; in Manual it is blocked.

The DAC can select the transfer switch for isolation and restoration, but the local logic transfers faster. The DAC normally recognizes the load has been accommodated locally and restores other stranded loads.

Transferring the load onto a feeder wouldDAC commands local logic to
exceed temporary capacity limits anywhere on the feeder or transformerManual.
not exceed normal capacity limits anywhere on the feeder or transformerAutomatic.
move load onto a feeder experiencing an eventManual.

To prevent the DAC returning the switch to Automatic, do not map the Automatic command from the transfer switch function block.

3Distributed controller functionality — connectors not in Extension

DA_DAC_INTERCONNECT pairs one controller's edge with another's. Each block pairs with exactly one block in another controller. A DAC may have at most fifteen. SEL guidance is to perform as much automation as possible on a single DAC.

Paired blocks share: connection ID, alternative feed availability, available capacity, present loading, abnormal conditions, fault indications, dead-line/live-line indications, poll requests.

A DAC considers sources in its own area and in directly connected remote areas, but gives preference to local sources. This can cause it to sectionalize more than capacity alone requires.

Best practices, in priority order

  1. Minimize the number of connectors.
  2. Do not place a connector at a line segment touching more than two switching devices — no three-way or larger interconnection zones.
  3. Place connectors near the end of the feeder in the normal configuration; ideally one side touches a normally open device. Secondary to rule 2.
  4. Avoid more than one connector per feeder in the normal configuration.
  5. Avoid placing a connector at a line segment touching single-phase operate devices.

Limitations

  • Both DA systems must use exactly the same versions of the DA and Adapter libraries.
  • Transfer depth calculations reset at the interconnection zone.
  • RTN is supported only if the normal open point is immediately adjacent to the connector. Otherwise RTN may produce no operations and must be done manually.
  • Load transfer and overload mitigation are not supported at or across interconnection zones.
  • The interconnection zone is always treated as the same source for settings-group coordination.
  • Two or more interconnections between the same two feeders produce incorrect available-capacity calculations and may prevent restoration near capacity limits.
In logs. SCADA reporting from a connector indicates only whether the remote system is eligible as a restoration source. In SOE and event logs the connector is modeled as a feeder source, a load-break capable "Source Switch", and a load-break capable "Load Switch" in series.

4Distributed generation not in Extension

The DAC is an automation-speed system and does not provide isolation or anti-islanding controls for distributed generation. DG automatic disconnection and anti-islanding must be performed by protection devices.

A DG connection is modeled as a point-of-interconnection recloser serving as a radial tap. The DG may supply power, consume it, or alternate. The POI is assumed to disconnect itself on loss of utility feed and not to reconnect or form an island.

Unmodeled

DG changes the effective load of its zone, so the DAC underestimates the actual zone load for restoration. Acceptable where the DG component is small or ampacity ratings are sufficiently overbuilt.

Modeled

Requirements:

  • All reclosers along the feeder should report real power with signs to indicate direction.
  • The POI must disconnect itself automatically if an event interrupts service at the point of interconnection. The DAC sends open commands if it does not, but these are not at protection speeds.

Functionality gained:

  • Zone loading calculated taking power flow direction into account, including whether the POI is supplying or consuming.
  • Restoration sequences computed assuming DG sources stay disconnected and offset no loading.
  • Optional close-block / close-permissive commands to the POI. The DAC applies a close-block if the POI is not connected to a utility source, or is connected to a feeder marked ineligible for that DG.
The DAC cannot command a DG point of interconnection to close. Only to block or permit.

5Loop feeder arrangements not in Extension

Loop objects are initialized with InitLoop and InitPowDir. Sustained loop operation is permitted between feeders modeled with loop-configured feeder objects, only if every switching object between the circuit objects is also loop capable. Non-loop switching objects may be used on radial sections branching off the loop.

Loop-configured switching objects require two directional fault indication inputs, and use the sign of the real power input to determine flow direction.

Will do, on a valid loop

  1. Identify line segments that experienced a permanent fault.
  2. Isolate them.
  3. Restore unfaulted segments left de-energized.
  4. Respond to miscoordination by isolating and restoring.
  5. Allow loop operation of up to three user-designated loop circuits, only if every device between the sources is designated for loop operation.
  6. Allow loop operation but never automatically create new loops.
  7. Allow automatic restoration from valid paralleled sources, only if no new loops are created.
  8. Generate overload alarms only for equipment or line sections with sources connected on both sides.
  9. Alarm on devices reporting fault indications in both directions.

Will not do

  1. No open-phase detection on line sections with sources on both sides.
  2. No loss-of-source detection on a source if a loop exists to another source.
  3. No overload mitigation for looped feeders.
  4. No operator-triggered RTN for feeders in normal loop configurations.

Assumptions

  1. Loop circuits have capacity equal to the sum of the capacity available from all directions.
  2. Additional load is split between connected sources in proportion to the pre-event split.

LoopConfirmTime (default 60 s) sets how long two feeders may be paralleled before a Loop alarm is declared.

6Redundant controller management SEL support recommended

A hot-backup pair running identical RTAC programs. InitRedundancy carries the primary and secondary IP addresses; each RTAC must hold one of the two.

  • Both controllers poll all data servers continually. Servers and concentrators need a unique server session per controller so DNP events reach both.
  • Feeder exclusions and device out-of-service statuses are automatically synchronized. Controls need only be sent to one system, either one.
  • Only one system is active at a time. The backup keeps polling for a faster transition.

Choosing the active controller

RTU votingThe data concentrators track how many devices each controller is polling and report which has higher communication availability. The higher one becomes active.
SCADA watchdogHeartbeat pulses or communication status from SCADA. SCADA must address only one system at a time. The most recently addressed system is active.
Forced activeSCADA controls force primary or secondary active. While forced, the automatic methods are bypassed.
A difference in SettingsVersion between primary and backup disables automatic failover.

7Block close not in Extension

Works with logic in local switch IEDs so system topology can tell devices lacking local synchronism check whether they may close. Breaker, recloser and switch blocks carry BlockClose and EnableClose outputs and a CloseBlocked input. BlockClose is pulsed until CloseBlocked asserts; EnableClose is pulsed until CloseBlocked deasserts.

This feature is functional even when the DA scheme is disabled.
BlockCloseModeBehavior
BlockCloseDisabled defaultBlock close commands are not used even if configured.
AllClosedTransitionSends block-close to any open device with a source on both sides. Blocks all closed-transition operations.
DifferentSourceGroupsSends block-close only where the sources on both sides are ineligible for closed transitions.

Distinct from the DG close-block in section 4.

8External control system permissives not in Extension

The DAC can request permission from an external system before executing a reconfiguration — for volt/VAR systems designed to work only in the normal configuration.

  • RequestPermissive (feeder output) — sends the request, commonly to command a voltage control system into a specific mode such as disabling voltage reduction.
  • Permissive (feeder input) — monitors the external system's state. If not configured, the feature is ignored.

Typical case: a feeder has capacity to re-energize stranded load but is held at reduced voltage. RequestPermissive disables the voltage reduction; Permissive confirms; restoration proceeds.

9Dynamic station arrangement

Substation bus arrangement changes are tracked through the substation model. Capacity arithmetic, the parallel-transformer rule, margin calculation and the block set are on Loading, Capacity & Voltage.
