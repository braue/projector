DAC — Operator Q&A & Troubleshooting

Control-room questions answered from the manual, plus the checklists for "why didn't it operate" and "why did only part come back"

Each answer is built from explicit manual statements — event and RTN acceptance conditions, sequence exits, the UFLS manual-operation rule, the definition of a feeder, Initialize behavior — and links to the page carrying the rule.

Where a behavior is the absence of a described mechanism — resuming after a reboot, rolling back after an abort — the entry says so. Confirm those two with dac_support@selinc.com before stating them to a customer.

1Events on top of events

QuestionBehavior
A second fault hits while the first sequence is still executing. New events are detected in every step, and "new event" is an explicit exit from the Reconfiguration step. The system re-evaluates and solves all confirmed events together. Sequential and concurrent faults are a listed capability. Two things abort outright mid-sequence: close-onto-fault, and directional fault discrepancy. See Event Sequence.
Two feeders fault at once. Reconfiguration waits until every feeder's event sequence is idle, then evaluates all confirmed events in one solution pass. Shared alternates are allocated by the criteria ladder, not first-come. See Reconfiguration & Switching.
An overload rides through a fault event. The fault wins. Overload monitoring runs only when there is no fault or open-phase condition on the feeder and no abnormal conditions. The overload is acted on afterwards, when its system-wide confirm timer expires. See Overload.

2Human actions

QuestionBehavior
Dispatch manually opens a modeled breaker or switch. Will the DAC restore around it? No. A device operating without a fault indication is a manual operation. No DA event. The area downstream of a modeled open device is not part of any feeder — a feeder is a source feeding zones through closed devices — so no open-phase pattern exists on the energized side. AR continues on the new present topology. See Source Events & Underfrequency.
Planned upstream or transmission switching kills station voltage. That is an event. Loss of substation voltage is an open-phase (dead-bus) condition. Once the ~60 s confirmation time expires the DAC moves feeders to alternates. The 10 s detect delay only rides through brief transients. Disable the scheme or exclude the feeders before planned upstream outages. See Open Phase & Loss of Source.
A hot-line tag goes on mid-event. Three outcomes. Event feeder, before confirmation: sequence aborts to Idle with Abnormal Failure alarms. Candidate alternate, during Update or Analyze: that feeder is dropped from solution consideration. During execution: the sequence can abort on AbnormalCondition when the reconfiguration time limit expires. See Abnormal Conditions.
Can I RTN with the scheme disabled? No. RTN requires the feeder Armed, no events ongoing anywhere, no abnormals on the present or normal configuration, and RTN enabled. See Return to Normal.
Operator disables the DAC to abort a sequence. Then what? The sequence stops. That is the designed abort, and the only one. No rollback of executed switching is described. Whatever opened or closed stays that way. Cleanup is manual, or RTN once re-enabled and healthy.

3DAC behavior

QuestionBehavior
Will FLISR ever reclose the device that locked out? Only when it locked out upstream of the true fault — miscoordination correction. It never re-energizes the faulted zone. See Permanent Fault & Miscoordination.
The DAC reboots mid-event, or while off-normal. Feeders come up in Initialize: internal variables and alarm conditions reset, configuration re-evaluated, everything re-polled. No mechanism for resuming a half-executed sequence is described. Expect the system as-switched with targets rebuilt from live data. Finish by manual switching or RTN. DAC_Enabled is never retained across a restart. See Feeder States, Arming & Reset.
The deepest device never reports its fault indication. Two cases. Comm-failed: the feeder is abnormal and the event is blocked. Online but the FI logic does not assert: the DAC locates the zone below the deepest FI it can see — one zone too far upstream, and isolation lands wrong. The design assumption is FIs asserting on pickup at every device that saw fault current.
Back-to-back events with no Reset in between. Reset is ignored while any event is being operated on anywhere in the system. Latched Command Failure and Close-onto-Fault statuses persist until a Reset lands. A feeder Reset only reaches devices within that feeder's normal extent, which after a restoration is not where the operated devices are.

4"Why didn't it operate?"

  1. Is the scheme enabled? DAC_Enabled is never retained across a restart.
  2. Is the feeder Armed? Check Feeder_<Bkr>_ExcludedByUnderfrequency.
  3. Is there an abnormal tag anywhere on the feeder? Check Feeder_<Bkr>_Abnormal, then the device points.
  4. Did anything lock out? No lockout = no permanent fault, regardless of fault indications.
  5. Open phase: was a fault indication blocking it? Any FI on the feeder suppresses open-phase evaluation. Is voltage discrepancy detection off, leaving open-phase detection disabled on a feeder with a suspect PT?
  6. Did Update or Analyze time out? _UpdateTimeout on the event feeder stops everything. _AnalyzeTimeout means the event was not characterized — check AnalyzeTimeLimit > OpenPhaseConfirmTime.
  7. RTN only: scope. Would completing the return need a device on a feeder that was not commanded, or drop load? Then no switching occurs.

5"Why did only part come back?"

  • CapacityLimit gives no reason. Check margins (the minimum along the whole path back to the source, transformer included), Level 1 voltage gates (AllowSelectLevel1 / AllowRestoreLevel1), nonpreferred-source penalties across a connector, and abnormal neighbours that removed an alternate.
  • The event report has the scenario detail. The alarm does not. Pair it with the pre-event report: a PT reading dead for a week does not appear in the SOE.
  • After any event the system stays off-normal until an RTN. RTN will not act unless it can do the whole job.
Load transfer and overload mitigation are not supported at or across interconnection zones, and transfer depth resets at a connector. Stranded load near a control-area boundary is often this, not capacity.
