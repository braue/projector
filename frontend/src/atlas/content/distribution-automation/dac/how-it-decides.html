DAC — How It Decides

From event detection to switching execution — states, sequences, event types, solution criteria, and mechanics

☰The rules, stated plainly

The mission

  • The DAC is FLISR: find the fault, isolate the zone, restore everything else. Protection always acts first — the DAC cleans up after lockout, at automation speed.
  • The network is zones bounded by switching devices, evaluated as radial feeders — one source per zone, always (loops only where explicitly configured).
  • The deepest fault indication picks the zone; an upstream lockout confirms it's permanent. Reclosing gets its chance before the DAC ever moves.

Behavior

  • Events only start on Armed feeders: scheme Enabled + feeder not Excluded.
  • Isolation switches open immediately at event confirmation — before the full solution exists.
  • One abnormal device makes the whole feeder abnormal, and abnormal feeders are left out of every solution.
  • Solutions are generated fewest-operations-first; the first complete one wins. Priority: temp-limit overloads, then stranded load, then normal-limit overloads, then switching count.

1What the DAC is

An RTAC-hosted controller with authority over a set of distribution feeders. It talks DNP3 to recloser controls, switch controls, and feeder relays — unsolicited events as things change, periodic integrity polls for health — and sends Open/Close, Reset, and settings-group-change commands as the automatic sequence requires (SEL Fast Message and IEC 61850 MMS also work). It detects and acts on five event families:

Around the core: SOE logging, post-event report generation and emailing, commissioning HMI with simulation mode, distributed multi-DAC collaboration, and hot-backup redundancy. Commands come from SCADA or a local RTAC HMI — SCADA is the preferred interface.

2The machinery: armed feeders and three sequences

Each feeder is always in one of three states — Initialize (reset internals, learn the normal arrangement) → Unarmed → Armed (scheme enabled, feeder included). Three sequences run on top: the Event sequence detects and confirms, the RTN sequence validates operator return-to-normal commands, and the Reconfiguration sequence finds and executes the solution for whatever the other two confirm.

The pipeline every event rides. Update exists so decisions are made on fresh data; Analyze confirms or aborts; the reconfiguration half solves and executes. ▶ Animated in the HTML edition.

  • Update: integrity poll of the event feeder plus neighbors out to the transfer depth, with a minimum dwell so fast trips (straight to lockout) still get fresh field readings. Timeout on the event feeder → back to Idle with an Update Timeout alarm; timeout on a neighbor → continue, but that neighbor is marked abnormal.
  • Analyze: confirms permanent fault / open phase / overload, or aborts (event cleared, abnormal condition, or analyze timeout). On confirmation the DAC identifies the sections in play and fires the isolation opens immediately while the solution is still being computed.
  • Reconfiguration: waits for all feeders' event sequences to go quiet, evaluates scenarios, executes the winner. The whole execution must finish inside ReconfigurationTimeLimit (default 120 s) or it aborts with the relevant alarm (AbnormalCondition, CommandFailure, VoltageLimit).

3A permanent fault, end to end

The canonical FLISR story on a two-feeder network. Fault indications (FI) assert on pickup at every device that saw fault current; the deepest FI locates the zone — intermediate devices don't have to see it. The DAC then waits: if reclosing clears the fault, no lockout ever comes and the sequence quietly returns to Idle. If a device locks out (LO), the fault is permanent and the machine moves.

Final state shown: R1 locked out, R2 opened by the DAC, R3 closed — the R2–R3 zone rides on Circuit B until RTN. ▶ Animated in the HTML edition — the sequence replays with narration.

Miscoordination: if CB A locks out instead of R1 (or in addition — the DAC forces a fresh poll on the first lockout to catch multiples), the deepest FI still picks the real zone. The DAC opens R1 and R2 to isolate both sides, then recloses CB A to re-energize the wrongly-dropped upstream zones and restores downstream from an alternate. FIs must be fresh (roughly the last two minutes) and configured to assert on pickup without requiring operation — that's what makes miscoordination detection and non-fault-interrupting switches work at all.

4How the DAC picks a solution

Load is bookkept per zone (current in − current out, low-pass filtered, memorized at event start), and margin toward any tie point is the minimum margin of everything back to the source — conductors, devices, and the transformer if modeled. Scenarios are built from isolation, sectionalizing, and restoration switches (TransferDepth on DA_MANAGER_AR sets how many neighbor layers participate — set it to 2 unless the scheme is tiny). Anything paralleling two sources permanently, or feeding from an unavailable source, is rejected outright. Survivors are ranked, in order:

  1. Minimize overload above temporary limits
  2. Minimize load left de-energized
  3. Minimize overload above normal limits
  4. Minimize per-feeder and total load restored by Level-1-voltage (depressed) feeders — gated by AllowRestoreLevel1 / AllowSelectLevel1
  5. Minimize switching operations (fewest-ops scenarios are generated and evaluated first; the first complete solution wins)
  6. Minimize per-source and total load on nonpreferred sources (connectors, buses with events on a neighboring DAC)
  7. Bias toward the normal source, then maximize the worst-case margin below normal limits

The best available answer may be partial restoration, or nothing — in which case CapacityLimit asserts, which means exactly "couldn't do it all" and nothing more specific.

Sectionalizing: R1 and R3 open together; R5 closes when R3 confirms open (Circuit C picks up 100 A); R4 closes when R1 and R3 both confirm (Circuit B picks up 200 A). ▶ Animated in the HTML edition.

5The other event types

Open phase (broken jumper, dead bus)

Loss-of-voltage pattern-matching, zone by zone from the source: dead at a device, dead everywhere downstream of it, healthy upstream. Guards stack up before anything happens: all PTs must have read healthy first, the LOV pattern must hold for the event-detect time (default 10 s), and confirmation waits out the open-phase confirm time (default 60 s) so upstream reclosing and external switching can resolve it. Any fault indication on the feeder blocks open-phase evaluation entirely — which also means an open phase that flashed an FI at inception but never drove a lockout produces no operation. Devices without PTs merge their zones into one extended zone for both detection and isolation.

Isolation is configurable: Isolate Open Phase disabled opens only the downstream switch, and only if an alternate feed exists (single-phase load keeps limping otherwise); enabled opens both borders unconditionally — choose it where three-phase loads are common. ▶ Animated in the HTML edition.

Overload

Detected in Idle from filtered max-phase current: above normal capacity = Level 1, above temporary capacity = Level 2, each with its own confirmation timer (OverloadConfirmTime1/2). A warning alarm asserts on detection; the event sequence only wakes when a confirm timer is within 60 s of expiring, and the fix is load transfer — closed-transition where source groups allow.

  • Load shed exists but SEL recommends leaving it disabled. If enabled, it waits ShedTime (set it longer than ConfirmTime2), sheds the minimum, honors DA_LOAD priorities (lower number = more important), and will even open-transition across source groups to shrink the outage.
  • The confirm timers are system-wide. When one expires, every currently-overloaded area gets operated on — even areas that weren't overloaded the whole time.

Source events

SourceEvent on DA_CIRCUIT — bus fault, transformer lockout, station protection — is handled like a permanent fault upstream of the feeder: open the feeder breaker (or the next device out, if configured to isolate the breaker zone), sectionalize, restore from alternates. A breaker that opened just before or after the source event asserted is attributed to it.

Return-to-normal is an operator command, not an event — it is covered in DAC — Operating It in atlas.

6Switching mechanics

  • Order of operations: isolation opens (already fired at confirmation) → remaining opens downstream of isolation → settings-group changes → closes → closed-transition opens as their paired closes confirm.
  • Settings groups: the DAC works out which feeder each device will land on and commands the matching group. Open devices change groups before closing; closed devices change after the close on closed-transition transfers, before on open-transition ones. No group configured for the new source = no change needed.
  • Close rules (open transition): no continuity to a not-yet-open switch-to-open, exactly one source on one side, no operation in progress on either side, live voltage on at least one side. Closed transition adds: both sources present with the same phase relationship and the same SourceGroup.
  • Parallel protection: in a closed transition the paired open is sent the moment the close confirms — and if that open ever fails, the DAC re-opens the switch it just closed rather than leave two circuits paralleled.
  • Command failures: three retries, then wait out the window; the failure is latched (Reset only), the feeder goes abnormal, and the sequence times out if it can't complete.
  • Load-break-incapable switches are never opened while connected to a source. They still serve as isolation points for dead sections, via a three-step dance if needed (open upstream load-break device → operate the incapable switch unloaded → re-close upstream). Sequences that would blink customers only to work around such a device are refused — including RTNs through a closed, load-break-incapable normal-open.

7Troubleshooting & fine print

  • FLISR quality is FI quality. Assert on 51 pickup without requiring operation; no ground-element-only assertion (downstream unbalance from an upstream single-phase fault lies); restrain inrush (~12-cycle pickup delay, torque-control for cold load).
  • Excluding a feeder doesn't just stop its events — it removes it as a restoration source for everyone else.
