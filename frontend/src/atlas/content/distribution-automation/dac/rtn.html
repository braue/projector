DAC — Return to Normal

What RTN accepts, how far it may reach, the four reasons it refuses, and why refusing means no switching at all

push, never pullall or nothingclosed-transition firstCapacityLimit

☰Core facts

RTN will never

  • de-energize load, or
  • operate devices beyond the allowed RTN region.

And it is all or nothing

  • If a complete sequence back to normal cannot be found, no switching occurs. Simple re-energizations are not performed.
  • Feeder RTN pushes open points outward to the feeder's normal extents. It cannot pull them inward from feeders that were not commanded.
  • Scheme RTN is an RTN command issued to every feeder in the system.
  • Closed transition is used whenever possible. Otherwise RTN proceeds only if open transition is enabled.

1Acceptance conditions

An RTN command is accepted only if all of these are true:

  • the feeder is Armed;
  • no other events are ongoing anywhere in the system;
  • no abnormal conditions on devices in the present feeder configuration or the normal feeder configuration;
  • RTN is enabled in the system.
The abnormal check covers both topologies. An abnormal on a device that is currently on a neighbouring feeder but normally belongs to this one blocks the RTN.

Commands: DAC_ReturnNormalCommand scheme-wide, Feeder_<Bkr>_ReturnNormalCommand per feeder. Both operPulse.

2The RTN sequence

IdleForced here whenever the feeder is unarmed. Armed, it waits for an RTN command. Integrity polls, abnormal monitoring and event monitoring run as usual.
→
UpdateIntegrity poll of the devices in scope. Ends when they answer, or on UpdateTimeLimit.
→
AnalyzeConfirms the command, names the minimum switch set, hands off to reconfiguration, returns to Idle.

Update — devices polled

Devices on the present configuration of the feeder, plus devices on any feeders impacted by returning this feeder to normal. A device timeout on a necessary feeder returns the sequence to Idle and asserts the Update timeout alarm.

Analyze

An abnormal condition on the actual or normal feeder configuration aborts to Idle and sets the Abnormal Condition indication at feeder and scheme level.

On confirmation the system identifies:

  • all normally closed (NC) switches on the feeder that are presently open;
  • all normally open (NO) switches on the feeder that are presently closed.

That is the minimum set. Reconfiguration may add switches to avoid leaving anything de-energized.

Two exits to Idle: an abnormal condition found in Analyze, or completion of the reconfiguration operations.

Where the open point has been pulled inward — an NC device open and an NO device closed, both on this feeder — a single feeder RTN operates them and completes. Scope only blocks when completing the return needs a device on a feeder that was not commanded.

4The four reasons RTN refuses

Three set the CapacityLimit alarm. All four end with no switching operations performed.

ReasonDetail
ScopeCompleting the return requires a device outside the allowed RTN region, or would leave load de-energized. No alarm of its own.
No valid alternative feeder
CapacityLimit		No feeder available to transfer load to, for any reason other than scope: insufficient capacity, insufficient voltage level, or an abnormal condition such as a hot-line tag.
Transition type not permitted
CapacityLimit		Closed transition invalid because source groups do not match, and open-transition operations are disabled in the configuration.
Normal open point not load-break capable
CapacityLimit		An NO point is presently closed and the device is not load-break capable. The DAC will not attempt RTN on the relevant feeders even if open transition is allowed. Opening it would require de-energizing and re-energizing both sides.
"If RTN is not attempted for any of these reasons, no switching operations will be performed. This means that even simple re-energizations will not be performed if the system cannot completely restore the feeder to its normal conditions."

5Transition behavior

The system is configured either to allow closed transition only for all RTN operations, or to also allow open transition. In both configurations, closed transition is performed whenever feasible, based on the configured source groups and the load-break capability of the devices involved.

In the two-feeder case above with both feeders commanded and matching source groups, the DAC closes R4 before opening R3.

If that Open fails, the circuits are left paralleled. The DAC sends an Open to the switch it just closed — re-opening R4. Command failure is declared when the expected feedback does not arrive within UpdateTimeLimit.

Settings groups are changed per each device's association with the source that will feed it. See Settings Group Changes.

6Variants and interactions

Settings-group-only RTN

An alternative mode changes only the protection settings groups to the appropriate group, operating no switching device. Set on the DAC Extension Settings tab with the Return Mode.

In a normal RTN, protection devices that are not transferred are left in their existing settings groups even if those are non-standard. The sequence re-groups only what it moves.

Single-phase operate devices

Once a complete switching sequence is determined, the DAC's first operation is to three-phase close every device with one or two phases open. This puts the system into the prefer-closed model the RTN plan was built against. The normal RTN sequence follows. Partially-energized zones are not blinked during the return.

Exception. A partially energized zone at a DA_DAC_INTERCONNECT location may have its energized phases temporarily dropped during an RTN, even with closed-transition-only RTN configured. Configure the switching devices bordering an interconnection point for gang lockout.

Across a connector

RTN across a DA_DAC_INTERCONNECT is supported only if the normal open point is immediately adjacent to the connector. Otherwise RTN may produce no operations and must be done manually.

Loop feeders

The DAC does not attempt operator-triggered RTN for feeders in normal loop configurations.

Load-break incapable devices

Never opened while connected to a known source. Eligible only when the surrounding area is disconnected from every known source. Usable as an isolation point for upstream faults and miscoordinations; never in closed transition, load rebalancing, or RTN. See Device Behavior Modifiers.
