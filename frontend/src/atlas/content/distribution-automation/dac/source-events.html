DAC — Source Events & Underfrequency

The SourceEvent input, the breaker-zone option, and the underfrequency statuses that block DA operation

per-feeder inputbreaker zone optionUF blocks everythingno restoration

☰Core facts

  • A source event is answered as a permanent fault located upstream of the feeder: isolate the feeder from its source, sectionalize, restore from alternates.
  • The input is per feeder. A substation may drive each feeder separately or reuse one point for all.
  • A source event asserting within a short time of the feeder breaker opening is credited with causing that operation. Sectionalization and restoration still occur.
  • An underfrequency trip means the de-energization was intentional. No restoration occurs.

1Source events

DA_CIRCUIT carries a SourceEvent input for conditions upstream of the feeder that require it to be restored by other sources. Asserting it means: isolate this feeder from its source.

Common sources: station bus lockout, station transformer fault/lockout, breaker failure schemes.

Response

  1. Open switches to isolate the source event from the feeder, typically by opening the feeder breaker.
  2. Sectionalize the de-energized load if necessary.
  3. Restore the de-energized load from alternate sources, subject to the usual availability and capacity rules.
Coincidence rule. If the source event triggers within a short time period of the feeder breaker opening, the DAC considers the breaker operation to have been caused by the source event and still performs sectionalization and restoration.

Isolating the breaker zone

The feeder device definition carries an option to extend the isolated zone to include the breaker zone in response to system source events (IsolateBreakerZone). Isolation then uses the next switching device on the feeder instead of the feeder breaker.

Adapter V2.10.3 fixed a defect where this option could fail to isolate the breaker zone for certain upstream events.

Point: Feeder_<Bkr>_SourceEvent.

2Underfrequency trip

Definition. A loss-of-voltage or isolation event is considered an underfrequency trip if the underfrequency pickup asserts for the most upstream device monitored by the system, prior to or at the same time as the event. The breaker trip and resulting de-energization are ignored. No restoration occurs.
Field note — in practice this is a rising-edge UF latch on the breaker/device coinciding with a loss of source. Open question: the effect of a UF assertion coinciding with other event types — permanent fault, overload — is not documented and not confirmed in the field. The manual describes only the loss-of-voltage / isolation case.

Enabling

Map the Underfrequency Pickup data point in the Template tab for the most upstream monitored device on a feeder.

If the behavior is not wanted, do not map the point. There is no separate disable.

3Underfrequency load-shed schemes

Case 1 — UFLS operates a modeled feeder breaker

Intrinsically supported. No configuration. The breaker operates without an overcurrent fault indication, so the DAC reads it as a manual operation. No DA event, no DA operation.

Case 2 — UFLS operates a device the DAC cannot see

An unmodeled low-side or high-side main, or an unmodeled substation. The discrete underfrequency input on every modeled device handles this.

If the underfrequency status of the most upstream modeled device asserts, all conditions that may trigger a DA event are ignored for a brief time. An assertion shortly before or after a loss of voltage results in the open-phase condition being ignored.

Driving the status: coordinated underfrequency elements — recloser 81U elements with no protection function, coordinated with an unmonitored upstream UFLS breaker — or transfer-trip-style reporting, passing the status from the UFLS device to a device included in the DAC for reporting only.

Feeder_<Bkr>_ExcludedByUnderfrequency reports that a feeder is being excluded by an interfaced underfrequency load-shed scheme.
