DAC — Overview & Scope

What the DAC is, the three sequences, the six triggers, and the vocabulary the other pages use

AR ≠ DFO3 sequences6 triggersInhibit · Bypass · OOS

☰Core facts

  • The DAC is an RTAC running the SEL ES DA library. Its function is automatic reconfiguration (AR) — FLISR.
  • DFO (volt/VAR) is a separate, separately licensed function. Nothing on these pages applies to it.
  • Protection acts first. The DAC acts after protection finishes, at automation speed.
  • Three sequences: event and RTN run per feeder and only confirm; reconfiguration is scheme-wide and is the only one that solves and switches.
  • The network is modeled as zones grouped into radial feeders. One source per zone, unless loops are explicitly configured.

1What makes the DAC move

Six triggers. Five are events detected from field data. One is an operator command.

2The three sequences

Event sequencePer feeder. Idle → Update → Analyze. Detects and confirms an event. Opens isolation switches on confirmation.
→
RTN sequencePer feeder. Idle → Update → Analyze. Confirms an RTN command and names the minimum switch set.
→
ReconfigurationScheme-wide. Idle → Analyze → Reconfiguration. Generates scenarios, ranks them, executes the winner.

The first two switch nothing except the immediate isolation opens. Each hands a confirmed result to the reconfiguration sequence and returns to Idle. Reconfiguration starts only when every feeder's own sequence is in Idle.

3Vocabulary that changes behavior

Three SCADA-applied device states. All three remove the device from AR calculations and stop the DAC commanding it. They differ in what the topology model assumes about its position.

InhibitUse the reported open/closed status for topology. Ignore the device for everything else. Not eligible as a sectionalizing or restoration switch.
BypassAssume closed regardless of reported status. Not eligible as a sectionalizing switch.
Out of serviceAssume open regardless of reported status. Not eligible as a restoration switch.
An abnormal condition is never declared on a bypassed or out-of-service device. Overloads are never detected on one. Inhibit carries no such exemption.

4Protocols and commands

DNP3: unsolicited messages on status change, plus a periodic integrity poll (IntegrityPeriod, default 300 s) to confirm each device is alive. Other protocols work if they use the same data types — SEL Fast Message, IEC 61850 MMS.

The DAC sends these command types and no others:

Openisolate, sectionalize, shed
Closerestore, correct miscoordination
Resetclear fault latches
Settings groupre-coordinate for the new source
Enable/Disable
Permissive · Auto/Manualonly when interfaced to external schemes

All switching commands are three-phase, including to single-phase-lockout devices and in response to single-phase events.

5Limits

  • The DAC operates feeder breakers and downstream devices only. It never controls transformer mains or bus ties, even when modeled.
  • It is not protection and not anti-islanding. A DG point of interconnection must disconnect itself.
  • It cannot command a DG point of interconnection to close — only to block or permit closing.
  • It does not build an operator HMI. It provides a commissioning HMI and SCADA reporting.
  • Sustained parallel operation requires both feeders and every device between them to be configured as loop objects.

6Functions in the library

Detection

  • Permanent fault, with or without miscoordination
  • Open phase — broken jumper, hanging conductor
  • Loss of source; substation dead bus
  • Two configurable stages of overload
  • Voltage discrepancy
  • Abnormal conditions — hot-line tags, supervisory disabled, non-reclose, control failures, comms failures
  • Multiple concurrent and sequential faults

Action

  • Isolation of faulted zones; restoration of unfaulted zones
  • Sectionalizing and restoration from multiple sources
  • Alternative feed selection on capacity and voltage
  • Overload mitigation by transfer, and prioritized shed
  • Operator-triggered RTN, per feeder or scheme-wide
  • Automatic disabling under underfrequency load shed
  • Distributed controller collaboration; redundant controller management
  • Looped feeder operation; spare breaker and transfer bus support
