DAC — Overload, Transfer & Load Shed

Two levels, two system-wide timers, how load is transferred, and the conditions under which the DAC sheds

Level 1 · Level 2system-wide timersmax filtered phaseshed not recommended

☰Core facts

  • Overload detection runs only from the Idle step, and only when the scheme is enabled, the feeder armed, no abnormals on the feeder, and no fault or open-phase condition detected on it.
  • The logic uses the maximum of the three filtered phase currents.
  • The two overload timers are system-wide, not per device.
  • The event sequence starts only when there is less than 60 seconds left on the confirmation timer.
  • Load shed requires transfer enabled, Level 2 confirmed, the transfer solution failed, and its own longer timer. SEL does not recommend enabling it.

1Detection

Three inputs: measured current flows, equipment and conductor capacity limits, overload confirmation times.

Measured currents pass through a low-pass filter that smooths step changes and jitter. The logic compares the maximum of the three filtered phase currents against the two configured limits.

Level 1 — above CapacityAbove the device's normal capacity. The limit the DAC keeps everything below when restoring.
Level 2 — above Temporary CapacityAbove the device's temporary capacity. After any non-overload event, the most load the DAC will put on the device.
Never detected onEquipment that is bypassed or out of service. Capacity values may be constants or REAL variables for seasonal ratings.

Detected once the condition has been present for EventDetectTime. Confirmed once detected for OverloadConfirmTime1 (Level 1) or OverloadConfirmTime2 (Level 2).

The two overload level timers are used for the entire system. Any area overloaded when the system overload timer expires will be operated on, even if it had not been individually overloaded for the confirmation time.

Sequence timing

Confirmation times are often long — a 12-hour emergency rating on a transformer. The DAC does not start the event sequence on detection. It:

  1. asserts a warning alarm for the feeder immediately;
  2. enters the Update step when there is less than 60 seconds left before the confirmation timer expires;
  3. moves to Analyze once the update completes;
  4. confirms the overload in Analyze if it is still present after the confirmation time has expired.

Every feeder overloaded at the moment of confirmation becomes an event feeder for the mitigation.

From adapter V2.10.1 the Feeder Overload Warning point reports overloading even when overload mitigation is disabled.

2Load transfer

The network is evaluated for alternates using the standard reconfiguration ranking. Closed-transition switching is attempted wherever possible; otherwise open-transition operations are considered.

Margin. Looking into the alternate circuit at the open tie point, margin = capacity − measurement, taken as the minimum of all equipment and conductor margins along that feeder from the tie point back to its source, including the connected transformer if modeled. The limiting element can be any apparatus or conductor along the path.

25 A overload at R1. A 50 A zone downstream of R2 is transferable. Circuit B has 100 A margin. ▶ Animated in the HTML edition: close R3, then open R2 on its closed status.

Execution: close R3 first, then send the Open to R2 as soon as R3's closed status is seen. If that Open fails, the DAC re-opens R3. Command failure is declared using UpdateTimeLimit.

Settings groups are changed for the new topology — see Settings Group Changes.

3Load shed

SEL does not recommend enabling or using the load shed behavior. The stated position is that operators or other human personnel should be responsible for decisions to shed customer load.

Load shed may only be enabled if overload load transfer is also enabled. It applies only when a Level 2 overload is confirmed and a load transfer solution failed to mitigate it.

The DAC sheds the minimum load possible to bring loading below temporary limits.

Source-group exception. The DAC never performs a closed-transition operation between circuits in different source groups. With no closed-transition alternatives and load shedding enabled, it will perform an open-transition transfer to a circuit in a different source group, to reduce the size of the outage a load shed would cause.

Priority

Priority is assigned with the DA_LOAD function block and considered when choosing what to shed. Smaller priority numbers indicate greater importance.
