DAC — Settings Group Changes

☰Core facts

  • The DAC associates each source with a settings group, per breaker or recloser.
  • Settings group changes happen at the start of reconfiguration, before switches are closed.
  • Open devices: the group is changed before the device is closed.
  • Closed devices: the group is changed after close operations for closed-transition transfers between feeders, and before close operations for open-transition / de-energized transfers.
  • A device with no group configured for the source it will end up on requires no change. It is left as it is.

1Configuring the association

Each recloser and breaker exposes SG1–SG6 Sources, filled with device names from the Breakers tab.

  • One settings group may serve several sources: device names separated by a comma and a space — Bkr1, Bkr2.
  • A single recloser may reference at most six sources in total across all its SG entries.
  • A source with no entry means no change is required for that device when fed from there.
Blank is not an error and produces no warning. A device with a missing association is left in whatever group it is in after the transfer.

2How the DAC decides what to send

  1. From the switches to close in the selected solution, determine for each switching device on the impacted feeders which feeder it will be fed from once reconfiguration is complete.
  2. Compare against the configured associations and each device's current settings group.
  3. Where they differ, send the settings group selection command.
  4. Wait for the status indication from each device confirming the change completed.
  5. Once all required changes have occurred, proceed to the switches to close.

The step runs inside the Reconfiguration step and is bounded by ReconfigurationTimeLimit (default 120 s). See Reconfiguration & Switching.

3The ordering rule

Stated identically in the manual under permanent fault, open phase, overload and RTN.

Only an already-closed device in a closed-transition transfer receives its group change after the closes.

In a closed transition the device carries load through the parallel, so its coordination cannot change until the new source arrangement exists. In an open-transition or de-energized transfer the device is re-sourced through a dead interval, so the group changes while nothing is flowing.
