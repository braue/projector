DAC — Loading, Capacity & Voltage

Where load numbers come from, when they are frozen, how margin is computed, and what the three voltage levels gate

☰Core facts

  • Zone load is arithmetic: current in minus the sum of the currents out.
  • Load values are memorized at the start of the event or RTN sequence. The solver uses the memorized values.
  • Margin is the minimum along the whole path back to the source, transformer included.
  • The load model assumes power flows from the source downstream. Significant DG must be modeled explicitly.
  • Voltage has three bands. Level 1 is live but sagging. Only Level 2 is unconditionally fit to restore from.

1Calculating zone load

Current is measured at breakers, reclosers and switches and reported to the DAC. Measurements are checked for valid ranges and passed through a low-pass filter to smooth quick load changes and communications glitches.

Memorization. The DAC memorizes the calculated load on all components in the distribution network at the start of a Feeder Event Sequence or a Feeder Return-to-Normal Sequence, and uses those values when evaluating scenarios. A zone de-energized by the event would otherwise read as zero load.
The calculation assumes all load flows from the circuit source downstream. Where that does not hold — high distributed generation penetration — additional data is required for the DAC to calculate load flow and present zone load correctly. See the DG section of System-Level Optional Features.

2Transformer loading

Transformers are the only devices for which current measurements are optional.

Measurements provided?What the DAC uses
YesThe reported transformer current. Includes load on unmonitored feeders.
NoThe sum of the current measured at all first downstream devices modeled as fed from the transformer — typically its feeder breakers. Load on non-automated feeders is invisible.
In a station mixing automated and non-automated feeders, the summed estimate under-reports by the amount on the non-automated feeders.

Parallel transformers

Total capacity at a bus is the capacity of the smallest transformer multiplied by the number of transformers in parallel. A 50 MVA in parallel with a 40 MVA gives 80 MVA.

Bus margin = calculated capacity of the connected transformers minus their present current. Station breaker statuses are monitored so the calculation follows the actual bus arrangement.

Blocks: DA_SUB_TRANSFORMER, DA_SUB_MAIN (transformer→bus breaker), DA_SUB_TIE (bus→bus breaker), DA_SUB_BUS, DA_FDR. Pre-combined: DA_XFMR_MAIN_BUS, DA_FDR_BKR.

The DAC never controls transformer mains or bus ties. Substation modeling adds information and constraints, not control.

3Capacity and margin

SettingMeaning
Capacity (A)Maximum load before Level 1 overload. The DAC keeps devices below this while restoring.
Temporary Capacity (A)Maximum load before Level 2 overload, and the ceiling the DAC will load the device to after any non-overload event.

Capacity must be ≤ Temporary Capacity. Both may be constants or REAL variables for seasonal ratings.

Margin. Looking into a circuit at an open tie point, margin = capacity − measurement, evaluated as the minimum of all equipment and conductor margins along the feeder from that point back to the source, including the connected transformer if modeled.

The two Level 1 switches on DA_CIRCUIT

AllowSelectLevel1 = FALSEThe feeder cannot be chosen unless it has Level 2 voltage or higher.
AllowRestoreLevel1 = FALSEThe feeder is only used to restore load once it has Level 2 voltage or higher.
Select TRUE + Restore FALSEA solution may be generated using a depressed feeder, but restoration waits until that feeder reaches Level 2.

Two ranking criteria limit depressed-feeder use: minimize the maximum load restored by any one Level 1 feeder, then minimize the total load restored by Level 1 feeders. See Reconfiguration & Switching.

Field note — the load on the segment being restored is not a condition of restoring it. A de-energized unfaulted zone is restored whether it measures 300 A or 0 A. Its load matters only as the capacity demand placed on the alternate. The four alternate-feed criteria are: voltage present on the alternate; alternate not abnormal; the transfer does not overload any device or transformer; alternate not excluded. None of them test the restored zone.
DAC_VoltageLimit and Feeder_<Bkr>_VoltageLimit mean restoration failed due to a voltage limit. CapacityLimit means no scenario restored everything. They are different alarms.
