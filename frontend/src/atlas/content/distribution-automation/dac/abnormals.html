DAC — Abnormal Conditions

Every condition that makes a device abnormal, what each costs, which latch, and how far the effect spreads

1 device = whole feederlatched vs self-clearingbypass/OOS exemptblocks RTN

☰Core facts

  • One abnormal device makes the entire feeder abnormal.
  • An abnormal condition is never declared on a device that is bypassed or out of service.
  • An abnormal feeder still participates in the Update phase, but is excluded from switching-order generation if the abnormal status still applies after the update completes.
  • A feeder that becomes abnormal during Update or Analyze is excluded the same way.
  • Abnormal on the event feeder aborts the sequence. Abnormal on a neighbour removes that neighbour as a restoration option.
Field note — abnormals attach to whatever feeder the device is in; feeder boundaries come from the configuration. Live voltage is not required for an abnormal to take effect. An open point affects the feeders on both sides of it.

2The conditions

Each marks the device abnormal, and therefore the feeder abnormal. Which are enabled is configured per device; the DAC monitors only what is mapped.

Hot-line tag

Applied at the local control pushbutton or from the dispatch centre via SCADA. The method of application is irrelevant to the DAC; it reads the indication. Self-clearing.

Reclosing disabled (non-reclose)

A recloser or breaker in non-reclose mode, locally or by SCADA. Self-clearing.

Switch Mode exception. For a device with an optional Switch Mode — a recloser in a settings group that disables element-based tripping — enabling switch mode makes the system ignore the reclosing-disabled indication.

Remote control disabled (device in local)

Supervisory/SCADA control disabled at the device. Self-clearing.

Communications failure

Declared if any one of these holds:

  • the device has been offline for an entire integrity period — no response to any poll request;
  • the device has not responded to an update poll or its retries within the update time limit;
  • the device has not been successfully polled since the DAC started.

Clears on three consecutive successful responses. Through an intermediate RTU, it clears on three unique watchdog values for the end device — message counts between the RTU and the device.

UDG threshold: a device reporting communication failure for five minutes, or for the duration of an event requiring an update poll of it, gets the communications alarm. Where a remote watchdog counter and a local message-received counter are both configured, the remote watchdog takes preference. Where a watchdog and an offline indicator are both configured, both must agree the device is healthy.

Command failure latched

A command was sent and the expected feedback did not arrive in a reasonable time — comms or mechanism. The DAC retries the control up to three times, then waits for feedback for the reconfiguration time-limit period after the command was sent.

Latched. Cleared only by a scheme or feeder Reset.

If the failed command was the Open half of a closed-transition operation, two circuits are left in parallel. The DAC sends an Open to the switch it just closed.

Device diagnostic failure

Any IED self-diagnostic alarm the DAC is mapped to monitor. Follows the device's indication.

Close-onto-fault latched

Inferred by the DAC, not reported by the device. After a Close command, declared if:

  • the device, or a device upstream of it, reports a new fault indication within a short period; or
  • the device closes and then opens again while an existing fault indication is maintained.
Immediately aborts any DA sequence in progress.

Latched. Cleared only by a scheme or feeder Reset.

Voltage discrepancy optional · self-clearing

Four detection conditions:

  • Across a device: one side of a closed device live, the other dead.
  • Within a feeder: an upstream device dead, a downstream device live.
  • Within a looped feeder: a device dead with a source in both directions.
  • Unknown source: a device not-dead with no source in either direction.

Detection enabled (EnableVoltageDiscrepancy, default FALSE): the alarm raises if a condition persists for an entire update time limit — UDG quotes 60 s — and clears automatically when the condition clears. The device and feeder are abnormal.

Detection disabled: no abnormal and no alarm, but on that feeder open-phase detection is disabled and the feeder is not considered a viable source. It still detects and operates on permanent faults.

Pole status discrepancy not abnormal

A ganged three-phase device reporting single- or two-phase open/closed status — mechanical failure, or a mismatch between intended and actual settings. The DAC logs it in the SOE and takes no other action. Ignored entirely for a device configured for single-phase operation.

Directional fault discrepancy loop systems

Loop arrangements require directional fault indications at every device. A device reporting fault indications in both directions implies faulty fault-indicator logic or hardware, or an evolved event that cannot be located.

Marks the device abnormal and immediately aborts any DA sequence in progress.

Protection failure (breaker failure) context-dependent

A relay detected a fault, attempted to trip, and the mechanism did not operate. Gang or individual phase — the DAC treats both identically. The relay's 50BF-style logic must latch and report it; the reset condition is the relay's business.

When reportedBehavior
No event in progress
e.g. maintenance testing		Abnormal condition on the device, making the feeder abnormal. Latched until the protection failure status from the relay deasserts.
During an event the DAC would act onThe DAC delays marking the device and feeder abnormal, and temporarily treats the device as bypassed for the event. FLISR proceeds without using the failed device. After the event the device is marked abnormal.
