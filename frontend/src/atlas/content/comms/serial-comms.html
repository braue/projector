Serial Communications

RS-232 point-to-point and RS-485 multidrop the SEL way — the 9-pin pinout, IRIG on pins 4/6, port settings that must match, cables, and fiber serial links

☰The rules, stated plainly

  • EIA-232 is point-to-point, single-ended, short: SEL rates it to 15 m (49 ft) in low noise. EIA-485 is differential multidrop: to 1200 m — with proper termination at the receiver.
  • SEL serial defaults are 9600, 8 data bits, no parity, 1 stop. Speed, bits, parity, stop, and the protocol chosen for that port must match at both ends, or you get silence or garbage.
  • The SEL DB-9 is not a PC pinout: Pin 1 can carry +5 Vdc, Pins 4/6 carry IRIG-B. Use SEL cables (C234A to a PC) — never a generic null-modem cable.
  • Past ~100 ft, or wherever ground potential rise is a worry, SEL's answer is fiber serial transceivers (SEL-2800 family) — isolation plus IRIG-B on the same fibers.

1Why serial still matters

Every SEL relay ships with serial ports, and decades of installed gear speak nothing else. The SEL-751 and SEL-751A each carry a front EIA-232 port (PORT F) as standard equipment plus optional rear serial ports; the SEL-451 has four EIA-232 interfaces (PORT F front, PORT 1–3 rear); the SEL-3530-4 RTAC has four serial ports that any of its serial protocols can be assigned to. Serial is the engineering-access path of last resort (terminal + SET P, even SELBOOT firmware loading), the transport for MIRRORED BITS, DNP3, Modbus RTU, and synchrophasors, and — via pins 4/6 — a way to deliver IRIG-B time on the same cable. It has no switches, no IP stack, no negotiation: one wire, one owner, deterministic byte timing. When Ethernet is down, the serial port still answers.

2EIA-232: one wire pair, two devices, eleven bit-times per byte

EIA-232 is a single-ended, point-to-point link: each signal is one wire measured against a shared signal ground, which is why distance and noise tolerance are limited (SEL: 15 m / 49 ft in low-noise environments; the SEL-2810 data sheet cites the "specified 50-foot limitation"). Three pins do the actual work — TXD, RXD, GND. Each byte travels as an asynchronous character frame: the line idles at mark, a start bit drops it, the data bits follow LSB-first, an optional parity bit, then a stop bit returns the line to idle. That framing is what the port settings describe: BITS (7 or 8), PARITY (O, E, N), STOP (1 or 2).

Async framing, two passes: a clean byte lands and passes the parity check; the same byte with one bit flipped by noise fails it and is dropped. Parity catches single-bit hits — protocols layer CRCs on top for the rest. ▶ Animated in the HTML edition.

RTS/CTS — when SEL gear actually uses it

All SEL-751/751A EIA-232 ports support RTS/CTS hardware handshaking, but it defaults off (RTSCTS := N, which permanently asserts RTS). With RTSCTS := Y: the relay deasserts RTS when it can't receive more characters, and won't transmit until CTS is asserted. The ports also support XON/XOFF software flow control regardless. Where the handshake genuinely matters: half-duplex media (radios, EIA-232↔485 transceivers) keyed by RTS — DNP3 serial sessions expose PREDLY/POSTDLY to delay data around RTS assertion (an EIA-485 transceiver typically needs 10–20 ms to turn from receive to transmit) — and MIRRORED BITS over a Pulsar MBT9600 modem, where the modem deasserts CTS to signal a bad channel.

The two odd pins: +5 V and IRIG-B

  • Pin 1 = +5 Vdc on SEL rear-port pinouts — it powers port-powered accessories (SEL-2800-series fiber transceivers draw <15 mA; SEL-2885/2886 EIA-485 transceivers run off it). Budget: 0.25 A total across all pins on the 751/751A; 500 mA cumulative across the RTAC's four ports (per-port Enable Port Auxiliary Power setting). On the SEL-451 it's jumper-enabled (JMP1–JMP3, one per rear port; default off). Front ports don't provide it.
  • Pins 4/6 = IRIG-B (+/−) — the SEL convention for demodulated time-code on the serial connector. 751/751A: PORT 3 (EIA-232 option) accepts IRIG on 4/6, e.g. from a communications processor over one C273A cable, or from an SEL-2401/2407/2488 clock via C292A + BNC tee; only one IRIG input (B01/B02 terminals, PORT 3, or fiber PORT 2) may be used at a time, selected with TIME_SRC := IRIG1/IRIG2. SEL-451: IRIG on pins 4/6 of PORT 1 only. RTAC: pins 4/6 of every serial port carry IRIG-B out to the connected IED.

3EIA-485/422: one differential pair, many drops

EIA-485 sends each signal as a differential pair (+/−); the receiver reads the difference, so common-mode noise cancels and the same data rate survives 1200 m (3937 ft) — SEL's stated maximum (751 and 751A manuals alike), conditioned on proper line termination at the receiver. Multiple devices share the pair: the EIA-485 standard allows 32 unit loads on one line (RTAC manual, citing clause 3 of the standard). Two topologies:

Two-wire (half-duplex)one shared pair; everyone transmits and receives on it, one at a time. RTAC 485 mode: tie −TXD to −RXD and +TXD to +RXD at each device onto the single +DATA/−DATA pair.
→
Four-wire (EIA-422-style)separate TX and RX pairs — client's TX pair feeds every IED's RX and vice versa. Full-duplex wiring, but no carrier detection: contention is still possible with unsolicited traffic.

The 751/751A DNP3 appendix (same text in both) is blunt about contention: unsolicited reporting on a serial network needs a half-duplex medium or carrier detection to avoid collisions — "EIA-485 two-wire networks are half-duplex; EIA-485 four-wire networks do not provide carrier detection." The relay inserts a random MINDLY–MAXDLY back-off after sensing carrier (RX data or CTS) before transmitting, and repeats unconfirmed messages until acknowledged.

One client polling three drops on a shared pair. Middle phase: with the far-end termination gone, the wavefront reflects off the open end and corrupts frames at every receiver — the classic "works at 1200 bps, garbage at 19200" failure. 4SEL port conventions: the 9-pin table you keep coming back to

SEL-751 and SEL-751A serial complements are identical: PORT F (front EIA-232, standard), PORT 2 (optional rear multimode fiber serial, ST), PORT 3 (optional rear EIA-232 or EIA-485 — fixed by ordering option), PORT 4 (optional comms card with both an EIA-485 port "4A" and an EIA-232 port "4C" — only one active, chosen with COMMINF := 232/485). The EIA-485 connector is a 5-pin plug-in (Euro) terminal block accepting AWG 26–14, stripped 8 mm — not a DB-9.

Beyond direct ports, the RTAC does Client–Multidrop Serial (one client entry per IED, all pointed at the same COM port, unique server addresses), Ethernet-tunneled serial through a serial port server, and virtual port-server modes mapping serial ports to IP ports.

5Port settings: five things that must match end-to-end

Both ends must agree on speed, data bits, parity, stop bits — and each SEL port additionally has a protocol selection that decides what the port even listens for. The 751 and 751A manuals' instruction (verbatim in both) is the whole discipline: "set the speed, data bits, parity, and stop bits settings to match the serial port configuration of the equipment that is communicating with the serial port."

  • RTAC side: each serial client/server connection carries the mirror-image settings — Serial Port (Com_0x), Serial Type (EIA-232/485), Baud Rate, Data Bits, Parity, Stop Bit, plus half/full duplex and pin-1 auxiliary power — and the web interface's Connected IEDs report shows them live per device with a green/red status icon.
  • Protocol mismatch is silent: a port with PROTO := SEL ignores Modbus polls entirely; DNP settings only exist once PROTO := DNP. Check the protocol before blaming the wire.

6Cabling practice

  • Use SEL cables by part number — the SEL-5801 Cable Selector picks one by application. The regulars: C234A relay↔PC DB-9 (crosses TXD/RXD, ties the PC's handshake pins; keep ≤15 m — the 751 manual prefers the double-shielded C234R, and calls for the R variant on MIRRORED BITS links), C227A/C222 DB-25 DTE/modem variants, C272A relay↔comms-processor, C273A same but carrying IRIG-B on 4/6, C387 relay↔SEL-3010. RTAC lists C234A, C273A, C387 as its popular serial picks.
  • Never use a standard null-modem cable — the SEL-451 manual makes it a caution. Generic null-modems loop DTR/DSR/DCD (pins 4/6/1) for handshaking; on an SEL port those same pins are IRIG-B and +5 V. The C234A already does the TXD/RXD cross correctly with nothing dangerous strapped.
  • Twisted, shielded pairs for 485: the RTAC manual strongly recommends twisted-pair cables (SEL-C698/C697) for EIA-485 installs to cut noise susceptibility; the 751/751A's 485 connector has a dedicated Shield terminal (_05).
  • Distance vs. speed: 232 to 15 m in low noise; 485 to 1200 m terminated; over ~100 ft, or where metallic cable is "not appropriate" (noise, ground potential), the 751/751A manuals point to fiber transceivers instead.
  • Grounding reality: pin 5 is signal ground (on the 451 it's also chassis ground; pins 5/9 are not a chassis-grounding conductor). The 451 caution spells out why copper serial between buildings hurts: switching interference plus ground-potential differences during faults can corrupt comms and damage equipment.

7Fiber serial links: the SEL-2800 family

An SEL-2810 transceiver plugs straight onto a DB-9, draws its power from the port (5–10 Vdc, <15 mA — pin 1, 3, or 7), and turns the EIA-232 link into a duplex fiber pair: full-duplex serial to 20 kbps plus simplex IRIG-B riding the same fibers. The MT unit transmits serial + IRIG toward the MR, which outputs IRIG on pins 4/6 or a 2-pin Molex (adapter cables C651 to BNC, C652 to DB-9) — so a clock at the RTAC end time-syncs the far relay over the data link. That's the standard pattern: RTAC + SEL-2407/2488 clock in the control house, 2810MT on each RTAC port, 2810MR on each relay in the yard.

  • Why fiber: isolation from ground potential rise, immunity to induced noise from control cables, no signal ground loops — the 2810 data sheet's opening claims, and the reason SEL suggests fiber past 100 ft.
  • Distance math: 200 µm multimode fiber (SEL-C805, to 500 m per patch), 650 nm LED, 9 dB optical budget. Max length = budget ÷ fiber loss — at a typical 12 dB/km that's ~0.6 km after splice losses. Link delay is negligible for SCADA: 50 µs + 5 µs/km (serial), 80 µs + 5 µs/km (IRIG).
  • On-relay fiber: the 751/751A's optional PORT 2 is a built-in fiber serial port (ST) good to 1 km and SEL-2812-compatible; an SEL-2812 on copper PORT 3 reaches 4 km. PORT 2 doubles as the IRIG path (TIME_SRC := IRIG2) and as the SEL-2600 RTD module link.
  • LEDs are the first test point: the 2810's Power LED lights when the port supplies enough voltage; TX/RX LEDs flash with traffic. Dark LEDs → host powered off, wiring error, or a port that isn't DTE-compliant — the data sheet's entire troubleshooting table.

8Troubleshooting & fine print

No response, dead silence? Work the 751/751A's own list in order: cable connected → cable pinout correct for this pairing → speed/bits/parity/stop match → then the SEL-specific one: the port may have received an XOFF and halted — type Ctrl+Q to send XON and restart. After that, suspect PROTO (wrong protocol on the port) and, on Port 4, COMMINF pointing at the interface you didn't wire.
  • Garbage characters at higher speeds only → framing mismatch (speed/parity/stop) or an unterminated/reflecting 485 line (§3). On Modbus, watch the relay's BADCRC/UARTERR counters climb — details in Modbus on SEL Relays.
  • Half-duplex contention symptoms — replies that collide with polls, intermittent CRC errors when unsolicited reporting is on: two talkers on a 2-wire line. Fix with the documented knobs: random back-off (MINDLY/MAXDLY), RTS turnaround delays (PREDLY/POSTDLY — allow 10–20 ms for a 485 transceiver to turn around), or move to a medium with carrier detection. The RTAC logs a SERIAL_BREAK_EVENT when its UART reports capture-data loss on 485 lines.
  • Ground loops / GPR: comms that die during faults or switching are the 451 caution in action — ground-potential difference across a copper run. The cure is isolation: fiber transceivers (§7), or the transformer-isolated SEL-2885/2886 for 485.
  • On the RTAC, capture before you speculate: the web interface's Connected IEDs page starts a per-device serial pcap capture (and on SEL-3555/3560/3350, R151+, an online packet dissector for SEL, DNP, Modbus RTU and friends) — see the actual bytes instead of guessing.
  • One IRIG input at a time (751/751A): B01/B02 terminals, PORT 3 pins 4/6, and fiber PORT 2 are mutually exclusive; TIME_SRC must name the one in use.
