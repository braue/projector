Redundancy

  • Redundancy is layered: switch-level (RSTP), link-level (relay failover, RTAC bonding), and network-level (PRP — two complete LANs; HSR — one ring, frames both directions). PRP/HSR recover seamlessly; everything else takes a switchover hit.

1Redundancy at the endpoints

Switch-level redundancy (RSTP — see RSTP & Topologies in atlas) protects the fabric. The endpoints have their own layer, and the options differ by device:

PRP is the only option here with zero switchover time — there's nothing to switch, both copies were already in flight. The cost: two complete, independent LANs. ▶ Animated in the HTML edition.

Diagnostics: the RTAC web interface shows PRP conversations by source MAC (PRP is Layer 2 — correlate with the ARP table below it), the next expected A/B sequence numbers (incrementing = healthy), and a LAN ID Error flag that means A-tagged frames are arriving on the B port or vice versa — a cabling or LAN-separation mistake.

The IEC 62439-3 vocabulary — DANs, SANs, and RedBoxes. A device with both PRP ports built in (an RTAC PRP pair, a 400-series relay in NETMODE = PRP) is a DAN — dual-attached node. A plain single-port device is a SAN — single-attached node: it can live on LAN A or LAN B (and only sees devices reachable on that LAN), or sit behind a RedBox — a redundancy box that does the duplicate/discard on behalf of everything plugged into it, making its SANs look dual-attached. HSR uses the same vocabulary, with the added rule that every node in the ring must speak HSR — non-HSR gear attaches through a RedBox, never inline. Note: these are the standard's terms; the SEL manuals in this folder configure DAN behavior directly (RTAC, 400-series) and don't document a standalone SEL RedBox product — plan third-party or check current SEL offerings if you need one.

On the SEL-400 series, HSR runs on the five-port SFP card and supports both station and process bus. (Animated in the browser.)

PRP vs. HSR in one line: PRP buys seamless recovery with a second complete network; HSR buys it with a second direction around one ring — cheaper in switches, stricter about membership.

2Troubleshooting & fine print

  • Relay drops offline for ~a second during switch maintenance → that's FTIME doing its failover job. In switched mode instead, remember the relay itself is now inline: powering it down opens the chain.
  • PRP LAN ID Error = TRUE → the A and B networks are cross-connected somewhere. PRP's guarantee depends on the two LANs being fully independent.
  • HSR ring dead after adding a device → something non-HSR went inline. Only HSR nodes (or a RedBox fronting the stranger) belong in the ring.
